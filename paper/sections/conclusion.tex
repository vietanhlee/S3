\section{Conclusion}

This study formalizes **specimen-level data leakage** as a major driver of performance overestimation in fine-grained visual classification. On the benchmark S3 wood dataset, evaluating identical feature representations and KNN classification under standard random image-level splits yields an inflated accuracy of \textbf{95.81\%}, whereas enforcing strict specimen-disjoint partitioning via our proposed protocol reduces accuracy to \textbf{90.87\%}---isolating a \textbf{4.94 percentage point accuracy inflation} (and a \textbf{6.35 pp macro-F1 inflation}) caused solely by dataset partitioning flaws.

To eliminate group leakage, we introduced the **Specimen-Centric Data Protocol** (SCDP) and the **CEGS-Split** optimization algorithm, which evaluates seven distinct feature-space allocation strategies per species to achieve specimen isolation while maintaining per-class balance and feature-space auditability. Attention map visualizations further confirm that specimen-disjoint evaluation forces deep networks to abandon non-taxonomic surface shortcuts in favor of genuine biological structures (vessel pores and axial parenchyma).

Future work will expand SCDP to cross-device and multi-source wood datasets, release versioned group-disjoint benchmarks for the scientific community, and evaluate group-disjoint data governance across medical imaging, metallography, and spatial ecological monitoring.
