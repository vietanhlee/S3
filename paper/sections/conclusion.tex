\section{Conclusion}

This paper addressed specimen-level data leakage (Same-Specimen-Picture Bias) in computer vision-based wood species classification. We introduced a benchmark of six streamlined splitting protocols spanning five distinct algorithmic paradigms on an 18-species macro cross-sectional wood dataset. We established the "Three Pillars Framework" incorporating 16 quantitative metrics to evaluate leakage minimization, OOD difficulty, and decision boundary preservation. Finally, we proposed a Multi-Objective Simulated Annealing Meta-Selector ($\text{CEGS-Split}$) that optimizes class-wise splitting strategies across an $11^{18} \approx 5.55 \times 10^{18}$ combinatorial configuration space.

Empirical results demonstrate that naive random image splitting inflates accuracy to a misleading $99.87\%$ ($SLR = 100.0\%$), whereas DataSAIL Specimen-level ILP achieves maximum leakage reduction ($L_{\text{DataSAIL}} = 4,841,999.8$, $MMD = 0.1199$). Our Multi-Objective Meta-Selector maintains 100\% subfolder integrity ($SLR = 0.0\%$, $CCR = 100.0\%$), achieves a realistic top-1 accuracy of $98.68\%$, and secures the highest hardest-class F1-score ($0.8148$). This study provides a rigorous methodological foundation for future benchmarks in biological and physical computer vision.
