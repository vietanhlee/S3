\section{Analysis and Interpretability}

\subsection{Feature Space Topology and Specimen Leakage Visualization}
To understand how specimen-level data leakage distorts feature space representations, we analyze two-dimensional t-SNE projections~\cite{tsne} of feature embeddings across wood species. Under naive random image splitting (\texttt{PP0}), images extracted from identical physical specimen blocks co-occur across training and testing sets. Because deep feature backbones memorize specimen-specific surface scratch patterns, specular reflections, and localized saw mark textures, test samples cluster tightly around their corresponding training counterparts. This creates an artificially compact intra-class feature distribution and an illusory high classification accuracy ($99.87\%$).

Conversely, when evaluated under strict specimen-disjoint governance (\texttt{PP10\_DataSAIL\_Specimen} and \texttt{PP13\_DataSAIL\_Meta\_Selector\_Multi\_Objective\_SA}), test samples originate exclusively from novel physical wood blocks. This exposes the true biological intra-species variance and reveals overlapping feature boundaries among taxonomically close sibling species (e.g., within the \textit{Afzelia} and \textit{Dalbergia} genera), explaining the realistic performance drop and providing a true test of model generalization.

\subsection{Pairwise Distance Distribution Analysis}
For $L_2$-normalized embeddings $\phi(x_i), \phi(x_j) \in \mathbb{S}^{D-1}$, Euclidean distance relates directly to Cosine similarity via:
\begin{equation}
d_{ij} = \|\phi(x_i) - \phi(x_j)\|_2 = \sqrt{2(1 - \phi(x_i)^T \phi(x_j))}.
\end{equation}

Under naive random image splits (\texttt{PP0}), near-duplicate image pairs originating from identical physical blocks generate an artificially sharp intra-class distance peak near $d_{ij} \to 0$, giving a false impression of highly separable species clusters. Under specimen-disjoint partitioning (\texttt{PP10} and \texttt{PP13}), intra-class distance distributions broaden to reflect natural biological variation across distinct physical specimens.

\subsection{Attention Map Auditing and Shortcut Learning}
Visualizing Grad-CAM~\cite{gradcam} feature attribution maps highlights two contrasting learning behaviors:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Random Split Shortcut Memorization (\texttt{PP0})}: Networks trained under random image splits focus activation on \emph{non-taxonomic surface artifacts}---such as mechanical saw blade scratches, sanding grit patterns, specimen edge borders, optical illumination gradients, and camera sensor noise fingerprints. The network acts as a "Clever Hans" predictor re-identifying known wood blocks.
\item \textbf{Specimen-Disjoint Feature Grounding (\texttt{PP13})}: When trained under specimen-disjoint governance, networks are forced to ignore block-specific shortcuts and instead focus activation strictly on \emph{diagnostic anatomical structures recognized by wood anatomists}: solitary and grouped vessel pores, axial parenchyma bands, and wood ray orientations.
\end{itemize}

This visual evidence confirms that specimen-level data governance encourages deep neural networks to learn anatomically grounded representations that generalize to novel physical timber samples.
