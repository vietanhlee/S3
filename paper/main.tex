\title[mode=title]{Addressing Specimen-Level Data Leakage in Fine-Grained Image Classification: A Case Study on Wood Species Identification}

\author[1]{Viet-Anh Le}[type=editor, auid=001, bioid=1]
\cormark[1]
\ead{anhlv.b23kh002@stu.ptit.edu.vn}

\address[1]{Posts and Telecommunications Institute of Technology, Faculty of Information Technology, Hanoi, Vietnam}

\cortext[1]{Corresponding author}

\begin{abstract}
Computer vision models deployed on physical specimen imagery (e.g., botanical identification, medical imaging, metallography) frequently suffer from catastrophic real-world failure despite achieving reported accuracies exceeding 95\% to 99\%. A primary driver of this deployment failure is that existing dataset generation workflows remain ad-hoc and unstandardized, storing cropped images in unindexed directory trees and relying on conventional random image-level splits. When multiple image observations originate from the same physical specimen, random splitting unintentionally distributes observations from identical physical entities across training and evaluation partitions, inducing severe \emph{specimen-level data leakage}. Under leaked splits, deep neural networks act as "Clever Hans" predictors, exploiting non-taxonomic surface scratches, optical vignetting, and sensor noise fingerprints to re-identify known physical samples rather than learning true biological features. To solve this systemic vulnerability, we propose the \emph{Specimen-Centric Data Protocol} (SCDP)---a generalized, end-to-end data governance framework encompassing physical acquisition logging, immutable metadata schemas, automated group-disjoint partitioning, cross-split proximity auditing, and leak-free evaluation. Within SCDP, we introduce \emph{Class-wise Embedding-Guided Specimen Split} (CEGS-Split), an automated algorithm that evaluates seven feature-space allocation strategies per class to guarantee specimen isolation while preserving per-class balance and evaluation difficulty. We validate SCDP on the benchmark S3 wood dataset (18 species, 5 genera). Under controlled classification experiments, switching from random splits to specimen-disjoint splits reduces accuracy from 95.81\% to 90.87\% ($-$4.94~pp) and macro-F1 from 94.93\% to 88.58\% ($-$6.35~pp), isolating the exact empirical inflation caused by data leakage. Diagnostic auditing via Grad-CAM further reveals that random splits conceal severe intra-genus failure modes, whereas SCDP forces deep networks to learn genuine anatomical structures (vessel pores and axial parenchyma). These results establish end-to-end specimen-level data governance as a mandatory prerequisite for trustworthy computer vision across all physical specimen domains.
\end{abstract}
