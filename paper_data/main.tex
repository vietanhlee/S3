\begin{abstract}
Rapid and reliable macroscopic timber identification from transverse end-grain surfaces is indispensable for combating illicit timber trafficking, curbing deforestation, and enforcing trade regulations under CITES Appendix~II. Nonetheless, deploying automated visual screening systems in practical customs and field environments encounters severe anatomical and methodological hurdles: pronounced \emph{intra-genus morphological congruence} among closely related sister species, \emph{inter-genus visual convergence} across divergent botanical clades, deceptive commercial substitution where unprotected padauks (\textit{Pterocarpus macrocarpus} and \textit{P.~soyauxii}) mimic endangered rosewoods (\textit{Dalbergia} spp.), and acute natural class imbalance (ranging from 219 captures in \textit{Afzelia pachyloba} to 590 captures in \textit{Pterocarpus soyauxii}). To address these real-world bottlenecks, this work introduces \textbf{IC4SDMacroWood}, an authenticated and forensic-grade timber image benchmark curated from an institutional collection of 260 timber species. The benchmark focuses exclusively on 19 commercial tropical hardwood species across 6 botanical genera of the family \textbf{Fabaceae} (\textit{Afzelia}, \textit{Dalbergia}, \textit{Guibourtia}, \textit{Peltogyne}, \textit{Pterocarpus}, and \textit{Sindora}) imaged at $50\times$ optical magnification. From an initial pool of 20 taxa totaling 7,854 macroscopic captures, an ambiguous entry identified solely to generic rank (\textit{Pterocarpus} sp., 576 captures) was excluded to guarantee absolute species-level ground truth, yielding a unified forensic benchmark of exactly \textbf{7,278 standardized macroscopic cross-sectional images} ($224 \times 224$ pixels at $50\times$ optical magnification). The benchmark contains 9 taxa governed by CITES Appendix~II and 4 endemic Vietnamese timber species (\textit{D.~cochinchinensis}, \textit{D.~tonkinensis}, \textit{Sindora cochinchinensis}, and \textit{S.~tonkinensis}). In addition to standardized imagery, the benchmark provides pre-extracted $L_2$-normalized ConvNeXt-Tiny embeddings ($\mathbb{R}^{768}$) and establishes a robust classification baseline via ConvNeXt-Tiny supervised with Multiclass Focal Loss (overall accuracy $82.16\%$, macro-F1 $77.30\%$). Furthermore, systematic metric learning experiments demonstrate that constrained geometric embeddings compress intra-to-inter Euclidean distance ratios from $0.6845$ down to $0.2810$, resolve congeneric ambiguities across $t$-SNE manifolds, and anchor Grad-CAM visual attention to authentic diagnostic vessel lumens and axial parenchyma bands.
\end{abstract}

\section{Value of the Data}
\label{sec:value}


\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Standardized Forensic Benchmark for Endangered Timbers}: IC4SDMacroWood delivers an open, rigorously curated collection of exactly 7,278 standardized macroscopic cross-sectional images ($224 \times 224$ pixels at $50\times$ optical magnification) across 19 commercial tropical hardwood species. This dataset is curated as a dedicated Fabaceae subset extracted from an institutional repository of 260 wood species. Significantly, 9 species are legally protected under CITES Appendix~II (notably \textit{Dalbergia cochinchinensis}, \textit{Dalbergia oliveri}, \textit{Pterocarpus erinaceus}, and all four \textit{Afzelia} species), and 4 species represent valuable hardwoods endemic to Vietnam (\textit{D.~cochinchinensis}, \textit{D.~tonkinensis}, \textit{Sindora cochinchinensis}, and \textit{S.~tonkinensis}). All specimens are validated against IAWA macroscopic anatomical criteria, providing an authoritative ground truth for fine-grained computer vision benchmarking~\cite{iawa1989,wiedenhoeft2011}.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Disciplinary Scientific Utility and Imbalance Testbed}: The release serves multiple interconnected research communities: (i) computer vision practitioners acquire a leakage-audited benchmark with group-disjoint partitions for fine-grained visual classification, metric learning, and open-set retrieval; (ii) wood anatomists and forestry educators obtain a standardized, provenance-documented photographic reference of anatomically challenging Leguminosae taxa imaged at $50\times$ optical magnification; and (iii) machine learning researchers gain a real-world case study for investigating specimen-level data leakage mitigation and acute class imbalance optimization (spanning from 219 captures in \textit{Afzelia pachyloba} to 590 captures in \textit{Pterocarpus soyauxii}) via class-weighted loss objectives or oversampling paradigms.
    \item \textbf{Granular Metadata and Provenance Transparency}: Going beyond traditional unannotated folder hierarchies, the repository supplies comprehensive specimen-level metadata (\texttt{metadata.csv}), 256-bit SHA-256 cryptographic hashes for exact de-duplication, physical-specimen group identifiers (\texttt{specimen\_id}), objective focus sharpness scores ($\sigma^2_{\text{Laplacian}}$), and standardized taxonomic maps (\texttt{label\_map.json}). This metadata architecture enables practitioners to audit every macroscopic image from capture source to partition allocation without ambiguity.
    \item \textbf{Leakage-Guaranteed Group-Disjoint Splits and Audit Verification}: The canonical dataset partition is constructed via a two-stage hierarchical meta-clustering protocol on specimen centroid embeddings. This design strictly prevents specimen-level data leakage ($\text{SLR} = 0.0\%$) while securing a 100\% Class Coverage Rate across all 19 taxa. Multi-metric audit reports empirically substantiate zero cross-split physical overlap, zero cryptographic collisions, and zero optical near-duplicates.
    \item \textbf{Pre-Extracted ConvNeXt-Tiny Deep Feature Representations}: To lower computational barriers for institutions with limited GPU infrastructure, the repository includes pre-computed, $L_2$-normalized 768-dimensional feature vectors extracted via ConvNeXt-Tiny. Researchers can immediately perform downstream clustering, k-nearest neighbor retrieval, and manifold analysis without retraining heavy convolutional backbones.
    \item \textbf{Operational Support for Customs Enforcement and Anti-Fraud Auditing}: The dataset explicitly targets commercial timber fraud where non-CITES padauks (\textit{Pterocarpus macrocarpus} and \textit{P.~soyauxii}) are deceptively substituted for high-value rosewoods (\textit{Dalbergia} spp.), providing an empirical foundation for training and calibrating field-deployable optical inspection devices for customs agencies and international forestry inspectors.
\end{itemize}


\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}


Illicit logging and transnational timber contraband represent one of the most lucrative sectors of global environmental crime, generating between US\$50 and US\$152 billion in illegal proceeds annually~\cite{dormontt2015,cites}. This uncontrolled exploitation accelerates the degradation of tropical rainforests, drives critical biodiversity loss, and deprives developing economies of vital fiscal revenues. In Southeast Asia and equatorial Africa, intensive selective logging has brought commercially prized timber species—most notably precious rosewoods (\textit{Dalbergia} spp.) and padauks (\textit{Pterocarpus} spp.)—to the brink of commercial extinction. In response, the international community has placed numerous commercial hardwoods under strict international trade controls through CITES Appendix~II~\cite{cites}. However, the effective operationalization of CITES mandates at maritime ports, border crossings, and customs checkpoints requires rapid, accurate, and non-destructive timber species identification at the point of inspection~\cite{wiedenhoeft2011}.

Traditional botanical timber authentication relies primarily on microtome thin-sectioning, anatomical histology staining, and high-magnification light microscopy conducted by certified wood anatomists in compliance with International Association of Wood Anatomists (IAWA) guidelines~\cite{iawa1989}. While micro-anatomical examination remains the gold standard for taxonomic adjudication, it requires specialized laboratory facilities, delicate specimen preparation, and days of expert analysis. Consequently, it is unsuited for rapid customs clearance where thousands of metric tons of sawn timber or raw logs must be screened in real time~\cite{ravindran2020,ravindran2022}. To overcome these logistical barriers, automated macroscopic wood identification using computer vision on transverse (end-grain) surfaces has emerged as a promising field-deployable alternative~\cite{woodreview,wu2021,fabijanska2021,figueroamata2022}.

Nevertheless, designing resilient deep learning systems for macroscopic timber classification is hindered by three fundamental anatomical and methodological challenges:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Intra-Genus Morphological Congruence}: Sibling species within the same botanical genus share close phylogenetic relationships, resulting in near-identical cellular topographies on transverse cross-sections~\cite{liu2025}. In the genus \textit{Dalbergia}, for instance, \textit{Dalbergia oliveri} (Burmese rosewood / \textvn{Cẩm lai}) and \textit{Dalbergia cochinchinensis} (Siam rosewood / \textvn{Trắc}) share diffuse-porous solitary vessel distributions, comparable pore lumen diameters ($120$--$220\,\mu\text{m}$), and undulating tangential bands of axial parenchyma~\cite{song2025}. Standard classification networks trained with conventional cross-entropy loss often fail to discern these fine anatomical distinctions because within-genus variance is substantially smaller than superficial surface variations.
    \item \textbf{Inter-Genus Visual Convergence and Commercial Substitution}: Evolutionary convergence within tropical forest ecosystems frequently yields convergent macroscopic anatomical patterns across phylogenetically distant genera~\cite{rosadasilva2022}. In commercial timber trade and illicit supply chains, non-CITES padauks such as \textit{Pterocarpus macrocarpus} (Burma padauk / \textvn{Hương quả to}) and \textit{Pterocarpus soyauxii} (African padauk / \textvn{Hương padouk}) are routinely marketed as fraudulent substitutes for endangered CITES-listed rosewoods (\textit{Dalbergia} spp.) due to overlapping reddish-brown heartwood pigmentation and banded parenchyma. Similarly, \textit{Sindora} species (\textvn{Gụ}) and \textit{Afzelia} species (\textvn{Gõ đỏ}) display analogous aliform-to-confluent parenchyma configurations, easily confounding generic deep learning classifiers.
    \item \textbf{Specimen-Level Data Leakage (Same-Specimen-Picture Bias)}: A prevalent yet critical pitfall in biological image benchmarks is data leakage across split boundaries caused by multiple macroscopic images originating from the same physical wood block~\cite{alturayeif2023,kaufmann2023datasail}. When images from identical wood samples are randomly assigned to both training and test partitions, convolutional models readily latch onto non-biological surface idiosyncrasies—such as sawblade scoring lines, micro-fractures from drying, or localized lighting gradients—achieving artificially inflated test accuracies ($>99\%$) that plummet catastrophically when evaluated on genuinely independent physical timber blocks.
\end{enumerate}

Overcoming these anatomical complexities and methodological biases requires two vital technical components: (i) enforcing a strictly group-disjoint dataset partitioning protocol where every physical specimen is entirely confined to a single partition, and (ii) leveraging deep metric representation learning to project macroscopic timber imagery into a constrained embedding space where intra-class dispersion is minimized while inter-class margins are maximized. The IC4SDMacroWood benchmark was created to deliver an open, standardized, and leakage-controlled testbed tailored for investigating metric representation learning under real-world conditions of congeneric resemblance, inter-genus visual convergence, trade substitution, and severe class imbalance.


\section{Data Description}
\label{sec:description}


The IC4SDMacroWood image repository comprises \textbf{19 commercial tropical hardwood species} across \textbf{6 botanical genera} belonging to the legume family \textbf{Fabaceae} (Leguminosae), encompassing exactly \textbf{7,278 standardized macroscopic cross-sectional images} ($224 \times 224$ pixels at $50\times$ optical magnification). The provenance, taxonomic vetting, and curation of the benchmark adhere strictly to international wood anatomical standards:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Institutional Provenance and Dataset Independence}: IC4SDMacroWood constitutes a dedicated Fabaceae subset curated from an extensive institutional archive of 260 timber species maintained at the Intelligent Computing for Sustainable Development Laboratory (IC4SD), Posts and Telecommunications Institute of Technology (PTIT), Hanoi, Vietnam. This collection is completely self-contained and rigorously vetted for forensic integrity.
    \item \textbf{Taxonomic Filtering and Elimination of Ambiguous Taxa}: The initial collection comprised 20 candidate taxa across 6 genera totaling 7,854 macroscopic captures at $50\times$ optical magnification. During rigorous taxonomic auditing against IAWA macroscopic anatomical criteria~\cite{iawa1989,wiedenhoeft2011}, entry \#10, designated only as \textit{Pterocarpus} sp. (\textvn{Hương Malaysia}, comprising 576 captures), was identified as being authenticated only to the generic rank (`sp.' level) rather than true species rank. To ensure 100\% unequivocal species-level ground truth and eliminate taxonomic noise, all 576 captures of \textit{Pterocarpus} sp. were completely excluded from the benchmark ($7,854 - 576 = 7,278$ images), establishing the unified canonical benchmark of exactly \textbf{7,278 standardized macroscopic cross-sectional images} ($224 \times 224$ pixels at $50\times$ optical magnification).
    \item \textbf{Natural Class Imbalance Characteristics and Mitigation Strategies}: The benchmark inherently reflects authentic forestry collection and trade availability distributions, exhibiting marked class imbalance. Image counts range from 219 captures in the scarce minority taxon \textit{Afzelia pachyloba} (\textvn{Gõ Pachy}) to 590 captures in the abundantly harvested \textit{Pterocarpus soyauxii} (\textvn{Padouk / Hương padouk}). In the held-out test evaluation, support similarly ranges from 30 images (\textit{Dalbergia rimosa}, \textit{D.~tonkinensis}) to 110 images (\textit{Peltogyne pubescens}). This realistic distribution presents an ideal experimental testbed for evaluating class imbalance compensation strategies, such as Multiclass Focal Loss, class-weighted optimization, and oversampling techniques.
    \item \textbf{Forensic Relevance and Legal Conservation Status}: Out of the 19 validated species, 9 taxa are legally listed under CITES Appendix~II (all four \textit{Afzelia} species, four \textit{Dalbergia} species, and \textit{Pterocarpus erinaceus}), and 4 taxa represent valuable hardwoods endemic to Vietnam (\textit{Dalbergia cochinchinensis}, \textit{Dalbergia tonkinensis}, \textit{Sindora cochinchinensis}, and \textit{Sindora tonkinensis}). Furthermore, the dataset explicitly targets deceptive trade substitution where non-CITES padauks (\textit{Pterocarpus macrocarpus} and \textit{P.~soyauxii}) are fraudulently marketed in place of endangered rosewoods (\textit{Dalbergia} spp.).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Macroscopic Xylotomical Characteristics across Genera}

Macroscopic timber discrimination on transverse end-grain surfaces relies on morphological features formalized by the IAWA Committee~\cite{iawa1989,wiedenhoeft2011}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Genus \textit{Afzelia} (Doussié / \textvn{Gõ đỏ})}: Diffuse-porous wood characterized by large, predominantly solitary pores accompanied by occasional radial multiples of 2--3 (mean vessel lumen diameter $\sim 180$--$300\,\mu\text{m}$). Axial parenchyma displays prominent lozenge-aliform to confluent morphologies, often coalescing into conspicuous eye-shaped halos around vessel apertures. Annual increments are demarcated by fine marginal parenchyma bands. Heartwood pigmentation transitions from golden-ochre to rich reddish-brown upon light exposure.
    \item \textbf{Genus \textit{Dalbergia} (Rosewoods / \textvn{Trắc \& Cẩm lai})}: Diffuse-porous wood with medium to large vessel pores, frequently occluded by dark gum-like organic deposits or glistening tyloses. Axial parenchyma occurs in fine, undulating, concentric tangential bands (1--3 cells wide), accompanied by vasicentric and confluent sheaths around pores (consistently highlighted by Grad-CAM diagnostic visualizations in Figure~\ref{fig:grad_cam}). Wood rays are very fine and homogeneous. Heartwood varies dramatically across species, from deep violet-brown in \textit{D.~cochinchinensis} and dark purplish-black in \textit{D.~melanoxylon} to golden orange-brown in \textit{D.~oliveri}.
    \item \textbf{Genus \textit{Guibourtia} (Copalwood / Mutenye \& Ovangkol)}: Diffuse-porous wood with moderately small to medium vessel pores evenly dispersed across the fibrous matrix. Axial parenchyma consists of continuous, regularly spaced marginal bands delimiting growth increments, accompanied by diffuse-in-aggregates parenchyma. Heartwood exhibits distinct brown-to-olive hues with dark vertical pigment striping.
    \item \textbf{Genus \textit{Peltogyne} (Purpleheart / \textvn{Hương tím nam mỹ})}: Diffuse-porous wood characterized by solitary vessel pores and occasional short radial multiples of 2--3. Axial parenchyma exhibits distinct aliform to confluent configurations with fine terminal parenchyma bands. The heartwood displays an unmistakable, vivid deep purple to violet-brown coloration, which oxidizes rapidly upon exposure, providing distinct chromatic and textural separation from other Leguminosae genera.
    \item \textbf{Genus \textit{Pterocarpus} (Padauks / \textvn{Giáng hương})}: Diffuse-porous to semi-ring-porous wood with moderately sparse, large solitary pores. A defining diagnostic hallmark is abundant, delicate, wavy, concentric axial parenchyma bands that alternate with fiber bands in a storied arrangement. Heartwood exhibits vibrant brick-red, golden-brown, or deep purplish-red coloration with a distinct sweet scent.
    \item \textbf{Genus \textit{Sindora} (Sepetir / \textvn{Gõ mật})}: Diffuse-porous wood with solitary vessel pores and radial pairs. A hallmark diagnostic trait is regular, prominent, continuous marginal parenchyma bands containing axial intercellular canals (resin canals), which are strictly absent in \textit{Dalbergia} and \textit{Pterocarpus}. Heartwood color is golden-brown to red-brown, darkening upon air exposure.
\end{itemize}

\section{Experimental Design, Materials and Methods}
\label{sec:methods}


\subsection{Physical Specimen Preparation, Micro-Surfacing, and Optical Macro-Imaging}
\label{sec:imaging_protocol}

Specimen preparation was conducted in accordance with established IAWA macroscopic anatomical criteria~\cite{iawa1989,wiedenhoeft2011}. Each timber sample was authenticated to species rank by verifying diagnostic morphological markers, including vessel pore distributions, axial parenchyma geometries, and ray configurations.

To achieve pristine optical clarity of diagnostic cell complexes and eradicate surface defects or sawblade striations, specimen cross-sections underwent a standardized four-stage surfacing and optical imaging protocol:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Orthogonal Transverse Surfacing}: Transverse end-grain surfaces were planed orthogonally to the longitudinal stem axis using an industrial sliding table saw fitted with a high-precision tungsten-carbide circular blade \reviewnote{Loại cưa bàn trượt và lưỡi cắt mẫu thực tế}.
    \item \textbf{Progressive Silicon-Carbide Polishing}: Surfaces were sequentially smoothed on a motorized rotary polishing platen using waterproof silicon-carbide abrasives across four calibrated grit gradations: P120 (planar leveling of gross saw marks), P240 (eradicating micro-grooves), P400 (intermediate anatomical finishing), and P600 (ultra-fine micro-structural polishing) \reviewnote{Các cấp độ hạt giấy nhám mài mẫu thực tế}.
    \item \textbf{Pore Lumen De-Dusting}: Microscopic sawdust and polishing residues lodged inside vessel apertures and pore cavities were thoroughly purged using directed dry compressed air jets ($6\,\text{bar}$ pressure) \reviewnote{Áp suất khí nén và phương pháp làm sạch lòng mạch}, ensuring unimpeded visibility of tyloses, vascular contours, and parenchyma sheaths.
    \item \textbf{Calibrated Optical Macro-Imaging}: Macroscopic imagery was acquired at $50\times$ optical magnification using a high-precision digital macro-imaging sensor system coupled with specialized macro optics ($f/8.0$, fixed working distance $15\,\text{cm}$, exposure time $1/60\,\text{s}$, sensitivity ISO 100) operating at an optical spatial resolution of $12\,\mu\text{m}$ per pixel. Specimen illumination was provided by a shadowless diffuse circular LED ring light ($5600\,\text{K}$ daylight-balanced, $4500\,\text{lux}$ uniform illuminance) aligned concentrically with the optical axis. Strict white-balance fidelity was maintained using an X-Rite ColorChecker target \reviewnote{Toàn bộ thông số chụp ảnh bao gồm tên thiết bị camera / kính hiển vi kỹ thuật số Dino-Lite AM4115ZT, khẩu độ, khoảng cách làm việc, độ phóng đại 50x, loại đèn LED và target cân bằng trắng - cần đối chiếu thiết bị thật}.
\end{enumerate}


\subsection{Image Integrity Verification, Three-Stage Quality Control, and Preprocessing}
\label{sec:qc_preprocessing}

To ensure that the benchmark is strictly devoid of corrupted files, out-of-focus captures, or non-taxonomic surface artifacts, the image repository was subjected to a multi-stage quality control (QC) pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Stage 1 (Bitstream Integrity Verification and De-duplication)}: All 7,278 macroscopic images were verified for bitstream integrity and valid JPEG header structures. An immutable 256-bit SHA-256 cryptographic hash was generated for every image file. Identical hash signatures were cross-referenced to eliminate bitwise duplicate captures resulting from redundant indexing or accidental multi-angle ingestion.
    \item \textbf{Stage 2 (Objective Optical Sharpness and Defocus Filtering)}: Because macro optics operate with an intrinsically shallow depth-of-field ($\sim 1$--$2\,\text{mm}$) \reviewnote{Độ sâu trường ảnh DoF 1--2 mm}, shutter vibrations can induce localized defocus. To ensure diagnostic anatomical features (such as thin marginal parenchyma bands and vessel boundaries) remain sharply defined, focus quality was objectively assessed using the variance of the Laplacian operator on grayscale channels:
    \begin{equation}
    \sigma^2_{\text{Laplacian}} = \mathrm{Var}\left( \nabla^2 I \right) = \frac{1}{H \times W} \sum_{x=1}^H \sum_{y=1}^W \left( \nabla^2 I(x,y) - \mu_{\nabla^2} \right)^2.
    \label{eq:laplacian_var}
    \end{equation}
    Image regions exhibiting $\sigma^2_{\text{Laplacian}} < 100$ \reviewnote{Ngưỡng phương sai Laplacian < 100 đối chiếu code lọc mờ} were flagged as optically sub-standard and excluded from benchmark inclusion.
    \item \textbf{Stage 3 (Diagnostic Anatomical Curation and Defect Masking)}: Cross-sectional surfaces were audited according to IAWA anatomical standards~\cite{iawa1989}. Non-taxonomic physical flaws were masked and excluded from dataset ingestion: (i) radial shrinkage cracks and seasoning fissures caused by specimen desiccation, (ii) peripheral bark remnants and edge chamfers outside the secondary xylem boundary, and (iii) vessel cavities clogged by recalcitrant wood dust.
\end{enumerate}

From the verified, defect-free transverse surface areas, standardized macroscopic images of $224 \times 224$ pixels were acquired at $50\times$ optical magnification (native $12\,\mu\text{m}$ spatial optical resolution) \reviewnote{Độ phân giải $12\,\mu\text{m}$}. During model ingestion, macroscopic images are normalized using ImageNet channel statistics: mean $\boldsymbol{\mu} = [0.485, 0.456, 0.406]$ and standard deviation $\boldsymbol{\sigma} = [0.229, 0.224, 0.225]$. Data augmentations during training comprise random resized crops (scale $0.8$--$1.0$), random horizontal and vertical flips ($p = 0.5$), mild color jitter (brightness, contrast, and saturation factors of $0.25$, hue factor $0.05$), and subtle grayscale conversion ($p = 0.05$) \reviewnote{Các siêu tham số data augmentation đối chiếu file utils/data transforms} to promote invariance to superficial surface oxidation.


\subsection{Leakage-Resilient Dataset Partitioning via Two-Stage Hierarchical Meta-Clustering}
\label{sec:meta_clustering_split}

A critical vulnerability in automated timber identification is \emph{specimen-level data leakage} (Same-Specimen-Picture Bias / SSPB)~\cite{alturayeif2023,kaufmann2023datasail}. When multiple macroscopic images originating from the same physical timber block are randomly scattered across training, validation, and test sets, deep neural networks readily exploit non-biological shortcuts—such as sawblade scoring lines, micro-fractures from drying, or localized lighting gradients—achieving artificially inflated test accuracies ($>99\%$) that plummet catastrophically when evaluated on genuinely independent physical timber blocks.

To completely prevent leakage while preserving the full spectrum of natural anatomical variability across all partitions, the IC4SDMacroWood benchmark employs a \textbf{Two-Stage Hierarchical Meta-Clustering Partitioning Protocol}:

\paragraph{Stage 1: Primary Specimen Grouping and Centroid Embedding Representation.}
Let $\mathcal{D} = \{(\boldsymbol{x}_i, y_i, \text{specimen\_id}(i))\}_{i=1}^N$ represent the benchmark dataset of $N = 7,278$ standardized macroscopic images across $C = 19$ taxa. All images originating from the $k$-th distinct physical timber block form a primary specimen cluster $\mathcal{S}_k$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}_k = \{\boldsymbol{x}_i \mid \text{specimen\_id}(i) = k, \, y_i = c\}.
\end{equation}
Each image $\boldsymbol{x}_i \in \mathcal{S}_k$ is mapped into a deep representation space via a feature backbone $f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\cdot)$ to generate an embedding vector $\boldsymbol{z}_i = f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{x}_i) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ ($d = 768$ via ConvNeXt-Tiny). To capture the holistic anatomical identity of specimen $k$, we compute its \textbf{specimen centroid embedding} across all constituent images:
\begin{equation}
\bar{\boldsymbol{z}}_k = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_k|} \sum_{\boldsymbol{x}_i \in \mathcal{S}_k} \boldsymbol{z}_i, \quad \hat{\boldsymbol{z}}_k = \frac{\bar{\boldsymbol{z}}_k}{\|\bar{\boldsymbol{z}}_k\|_2},
\label{eq:specimen_centroid}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}_k$ represents the $L_2$-normalized centroid vector for physical specimen $k$.

\paragraph{Stage 2: Secondary Agglomerative Meta-Clustering on Centroids.}
Within each species class $c$, individual physical wood blocks display natural intra-species variability attributable to tree maturity, growth rate, and sapwood-heartwood transitions. Naive random specimen assignment risks allocating all dark-pigmented or anomalous specimens into a single partition. To prevent this distributional skew, an agglomerative meta-clustering step is executed on the specimen centroids $\{\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}_k\}_{k \in \mathcal{K}_c}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}_{c,1}, \mathcal{C}_{c,2}, \dots, \mathcal{C}_{c,M_c} = \text{AgglomerativeClustering}\left(\{\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}_k\}_{k \in \mathcal{K}_c}, \, \text{affinity}=\text{cosine}, \, \text{linkage}=\text{average}\right),
\end{equation}
partitioning the physical specimens of class $c$ into $M_c$ distinct morphological meta-clusters using cosine dissimilarity:
\begin{equation}
D_{\text{cosine}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}_k, \hat{\boldsymbol{z}}_j) = 1 - \frac{\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}_k \cdot \hat{\boldsymbol{z}}_j}{\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}_k\|_2 \|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}_j\|_2}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Stage 3: Stratified Group-Disjoint Partition Allocation.}
Specimens from each meta-cluster $\mathcal{C}_{c,m}$ are systematically allocated to the training ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}$), validation ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}}$), and test ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{test}}$) partitions (4,960 : 1,253 : 1,065 images, totaling 7,278) \reviewnote{Đối chiếu số lượng phân bổ Train/Val}, strictly adhering to three governance conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Strict Specimen-Disjoint Boundary}: Every primary specimen cluster $\mathcal{S}_k$ is assigned in its entirety to exactly one partition:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S}_k \cap \mathcal{S}_j = \emptyset \quad (\forall k \neq j), \quad \mathcal{D}_{\text{split1}} \cap \mathcal{D}_{\text{split2}} = \emptyset \quad (\forall \text{split1} \neq \text{split2}).
    \end{equation}
    No physical specimen contributes images to more than one partition.
    \item \textbf{Cluster-Stratified Morphological Diversity}: By sampling specimens across all secondary meta-clusters, each partition receives an anatomically representative spectrum of heartwood colors, pore densities, and growth increments.
    \item \textbf{Full Class Coverage}: All 19 species are guaranteed representation in all three partitions ($100\%$ Class Coverage Rate), preventing zero-shot evaluation anomalies on the validation and test splits.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Classification Baseline Performance}
\label{sec:baseline_performance}

We assess the modernized convolutional architecture ConvNeXt-Tiny~\cite{convnext} as the foundational classification and metric representation backbone. ConvNeXt-Tiny integrates key architectural advantages modernizing classic ConvNets—such as $7 \times 7$ depthwise separable convolutions, an inverted bottleneck design, Gaussian Error Linear Units (GELU), and LayerNorm normalization—yielding strong representational capacity while preserving linear inference efficiency. Due to the pronounced fine-grained nature of timber taxa and the acute natural class imbalance inherent to forestry field collections (most critically exemplified by the minority taxon \textit{Afzelia pachyloba} comprising only 219 images compared to 590 images in \textit{Pterocarpus soyauxii}), naive cross-entropy training is vulnerable to majority-class gradient bias. To address this class imbalance without requiring synthetic oversampling, the backbone was optimized under Multiclass Focal Loss~\cite{focal} with dynamic modulation and class-weighting factors:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{Focal}}(p_t) = -\alpha_t (1 - p_t)^\gamma \log(p_t),
\label{eq:focal}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma = 2.0$ represents the focusing parameter down-weighting well-classified easy background examples, $\alpha_t \in (0, 1]$ provides class-balanced inverse frequency weighting to counteract class imbalance, and $p_t \in [0, 1]$ signifies the model's estimated posterior probability for the ground-truth taxon. The network was trained using the AdamW optimizer with cosine annealing decay over 20 epochs with a mini-batch size of 32 on dual NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPUs. Checkpoint selection was governed strictly by validation macro-F1.

\subsection{Deep Metric Representation Learning via Semi-Hard Triplet Loss}
\label{sec:metric_learning}

To resolve congeneric morphological ambiguities within \textit{Afzelia} and \textit{Dalbergia} and counteract inter-genus visual mimicry where unprotected padauks (\textit{Pterocarpus} spp.) deceptively simulate endangered rosewoods (\textit{Dalbergia} spp.), we evaluate deep metric representation learning on the ConvNeXt-Tiny feature backbone ($d = 768$). Specifically, the feature space is optimized via \textbf{Semi-Hard Triplet Loss} with online negative mining~\cite{facenet} to construct a constrained $L_2$-normalized hyperspherical manifold where intra-species distances are compressed and inter-species margins are strictly expanded:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Triplet Loss Formulation}: Given an anchor image embedding $\boldsymbol{z}_a$, a positive instance $\boldsymbol{z}_p$ belonging to the same botanical species ($y_a = y_p$), and a negative instance $\boldsymbol{z}_n$ from a distinct species ($y_a \neq y_n$), the metric objective penalizes distance violations across triplets:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Triplet}}(\boldsymbol{z}_a, \boldsymbol{z}_p, \boldsymbol{z}_n) = \max\left(0, \|\boldsymbol{z}_a - \boldsymbol{z}_p\|_2^2 - \|\boldsymbol{z}_a - \boldsymbol{z}_n\|_2^2 + \alpha\right),
    \label{eq:triplet}
    \end{equation}
    where $\alpha = 0.50$ designates the Euclidean distance margin constraint on the $L_2$-normalized unit hypersphere ($\|\boldsymbol{z}\|_2 = 1$, where $\|\boldsymbol{z}_i - \boldsymbol{z}_j\|_2^2 = 2 - 2\langle \boldsymbol{z}_i, \boldsymbol{z}_j \rangle \in [0, 4]$).
    \item \textbf{Online Semi-Hard Negative Mining}: Conventional random or offline triplet sampling suffers from severe gradient vanishing because the vast majority of triplets quickly satisfy the margin constraint ($\|\boldsymbol{z}_a - \boldsymbol{z}_p\|_2^2 + \alpha < \|\boldsymbol{z}_a - \boldsymbol{z}_n\|_2^2$), generating zero loss ($L=0$). Conversely, selecting the globally hardest negatives early in training can destabilize optimization and induce catastrophic representation collapse. To overcome these limitations, we implement \textbf{Online Semi-Hard Negative Mining}~\cite{facenet}. Within each mini-batch constructed via a $PK$-sampler ($P = 19$ classes, $K = 4$ samples per class, yielding batch size $B = 76$), we dynamically mine negative candidates satisfying the semi-hard condition:
    \begin{equation}
    \|\boldsymbol{z}_a - \boldsymbol{z}_p\|_2^2 < \|\boldsymbol{z}_a - \boldsymbol{z}_n\|_2^2 < \|\boldsymbol{z}_a - \boldsymbol{z}_p\|_2^2 + \alpha.
    \label{eq:semihard_condition}
    \end{equation}
    These semi-hard negatives lie further from the anchor than the positive sample yet remain within the margin penalty boundary, supplying stable, high-utility gradients that progressively condense intra-taxon clusters while separating congeneric species boundaries.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Controlled Fairness of the Experimental Benchmark}
\label{sec:fairness}

In adherence to rigorous empirical standards~\cite{convnext,ravindran2020}, comparative evaluations were conducted under strictly controlled experimental conditions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Unified Objective Formulation}: The ConvNeXt-Tiny classification backbone is evaluated under a standardized Multiclass Focal Loss formulation ($\alpha = 0.25, \gamma = 2.0$) with cosine annealing decay.
    \item \textbf{Strict Backbone Parity}: Both the supervised classification baselines and the Semi-Hard Triplet metric representation model utilize the identical ConvNeXt-Tiny feature backbone ($d = 768$), isolating the geometric impact of the objective on manifold structure.
    \item \textbf{Aversion to Model-Specific Overfitting}: Hyperparameters follow established literature benchmarks rather than architecture-specific grid optimization, delivering reproducible and conservative baseline metrics.
\end{itemize}


\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

While IC4SDMacroWood delivers an authoritative, forensic-grade macroscopic dataset, several practical limitations should be considered:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Controlled Laboratory Acquisition versus Field Deployment}: All imagery was acquired in a stabilized laboratory setup on polished end-grain surfaces under calibrated LED ring illumination. Real-world field inspections (e.g., at log landings or port terminals) must handle fluctuating ambient sunlight, varying moisture contents, surface dirt, and coarse chainsaw cuts, which can degrade image clarity.
    \item \textbf{Taxonomic and Biogeographic Scope}: The 19 benchmark taxa represent key commercial and CITES-listed tropical hardwoods primarily native to Southeast Asia and selected African trading regions. Expanding the repository to incorporate Neotropical hardwoods (e.g., Amazonian timber species) and temperate softwoods remains an important future direction.
    \item \textbf{Single Anatomical Plane (Transverse View)}: The dataset is currently restricted to transverse (end-grain) surfaces. Although the transverse plane provides the richest diagnostic morphology in wood anatomy, integrating longitudinal tangential and radial surfaces~\cite{rosadasilva2022} could supply complementary discriminatory cues for complex congeneric pairs.
    \item \textbf{Edge Hardware Deployment}: Deploying the ConvNeXt-Tiny backbone on resource-constrained hand-held inspection devices for real-time field operations requires further exploration into post-training INT8 quantization, network pruning, and knowledge distillation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Recommended Data Use, Reporting Policy, and Code Availability}
\label{sec:recommended_reporting}

To promote methodological rigor and prevent optimistic evaluation biases across future benchmarking studies, we outline the following reporting and dissemination recommendations:

\paragraph{Recommended Reporting Policy.}
When reporting timber identification metrics on IC4SDMacroWood, researchers are strongly encouraged to:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item Utilize the canonical two-stage meta-clustered partition manifest (\texttt{splits/split\_canonical.csv}) rather than naive random splitting, thereby preserving strict specimen-disjoint isolation ($\text{SLR} = 0.0\%$) and preventing data leakage.
    \item Report both overall accuracy and macro-averaged F1-scores, accompanied by per-species classification reports, to highlight performance disparities among congeneric sister species (particularly within \textit{Afzelia} and \textit{Dalbergia}).
    \item Explicitly specify hardware configurations, precision modes (FP16/FP32), random initialization seeds, and training durations to facilitate exact experimental replication.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Data Versioning Policy.}
The benchmark repository adheres to semantic versioning. The current release (v1.0.0) is permanently preserved on the Zenodo Scientific Archive with an immutable DOI. Subsequent iterations—such as the integration of additional botanical taxa or multi-planar longitudinal views—will be tagged under incremented version numbers, accompanied by updated split manifests and cryptographic audit checksums.

All data curation pipelines, quality control routines, two-stage meta-clustering partition generators, leakage audit scripts, and training implementations for the ConvNeXt-Tiny baseline and metric representation learning are openly available in the GitHub repository: \url{https://github.com/vietanhlee/S3_paper}.
