\begin{abstract}
IC4SDMacroWood is an authenticated macroscopic transverse-section image dataset curated for fine-grained identification of commercially significant tropical hardwoods and CITES-regulated timber. The repository comprises 6,414 standardized cross-sectional captures ($224 \times 224$ pixels, $50\times$ optical magnification, spatial resolution of $12\,\mu\text{m}$) across 19 hardwood species from 6 botanical genera in the legume family Fabaceae, including 9 taxa strictly regulated under CITES Appendix~II and 4 high-value timber species endemic to Vietnam (\textit{Dalbergia cochinchinensis}, \textit{Dalbergia tonkinensis}, \textit{Sindora cochinchinensis}, and \textit{Sindora tonkinensis}). Transverse wood specimens were planed orthogonally, polished across progressive silicon-carbide grit gradations (P120--P600), de-dusted with compressed air, and imaged under calibrated daylight-balanced ($5600\,\text{K}$) diffuse LED illumination. The release package includes raw images, structured metadata tables (\texttt{metadata.csv}), label mappings (\texttt{label\_map.json}), bitwise SHA-256 cryptographic checksums, Laplacian focus sharpness scores, pre-computed 768-dimensional deep feature embeddings, and a governed partition manifest ($N_{\text{train}} = 3,959$, $N_{\text{val}} = 1,265$, $N_{\text{test}} = 1,190$) preserving complete 100\% class coverage across all 19 taxa with controlled specimen boundaries ($\text{SLR} = 30.6\%$) alongside zero cross-split duplicate captures verified via cryptographic hashing ($\text{SHA-256 Overlap} = 0$). Technical-validation baselines using the ConvNeXt-Tiny architecture are provided to establish dataset usability: a supervised classification model under Multiclass Focal Loss ($\alpha=0.25, \gamma=2.0$) attaining $90.42\%$ top-1 test accuracy ($86.80\%$ macro-averaged F1, $88.82\%$ weighted F1 across $1,190$ test captures) and a deep metric representation learning framework via Semi-Hard Triplet Loss achieving a Silhouette score of $0.7538$, a Davies-Bouldin Index of $0.5671$, and $99.92\%$ Recall@1 nearest-neighbor retrieval. The dataset is intended to support reproducible machine learning benchmarking, forensic wood anatomy, and automated timber trade compliance. All data files, split manifests, and reproducible code are openly accessible at \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14892180}{10.5281/zenodo.14892180}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Value of the Data}
\label{sec:value}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Authoritative Forensic Reference for CITES-Regulated Hardwoods}: IC4SDMacroWood delivers an open, authenticated repository of standardized macroscopic cross-sectional captures across 19 commercially significant tropical hardwood species in the family Fabaceae. Incorporating 9 taxa strictly regulated under CITES Appendix~II~\cite{cites} (notably within the botanical genera \textit{Afzelia}, \textit{Dalbergia}, and \textit{Pterocarpus}) and 4 high-value timber species endemic to Vietnam (\textit{Dalbergia cochinchinensis}, \textit{Dalbergia tonkinensis}, \textit{Sindora cochinchinensis}, and \textit{Sindora tonkinensis}), the dataset serves as an authoritative forensic reference for customs inspections and timber trade compliance.
    \item \textbf{Rapid Screening Alternative to Destructive Microtomy}: Traditional xylotomical identification requires specialized laboratory microtomy (chemical softening, micro-sectioning, and microscopic IAWA feature analysis taking 2--5 business days~\cite{dormontt2015,wiedenhoeft2011,iawa1989}). IC4SDMacroWood provides standardized macroscopic reference imagery on transverse end-grain surfaces (where surface preparation and optical imaging require only minutes), establishing an accessible, non-destructive screening testbed for customs checkpoints prior to formal laboratory confirmation.
    \item \textbf{Governed Partition Integrity and Complete Class Coverage}: All 6,414 images are partitioned into standardized subsets ($N_{\text{train}} = 3,959$, $N_{\text{val}} = 1,265$, $N_{\text{test}} = 1,190$) preserving a 100\% Class Coverage Rate ($\text{CCR} = 100.0\%$, 19/19 taxa) across all partitions while maintaining governed specimen boundaries ($\text{SLR} = 30.6\%$) to avoid minority-class starvation, alongside zero cross-split duplicate captures verified via cryptographic hashing ($\text{SHA-256 Overlap} = 0$).
    \item \textbf{High-Fidelity Optical Standardization}: Transverse end-grain surfaces were polished across progressive grit gradations (P120--P600) and imaged under calibrated $50\times$ optical magnification ($12\,\mu\text{m/px}$) with daylight-balanced ($5600\,\text{K}$) diffuse circular LED lighting, resolving minute vessel-ray anatomical features that are obscured in low-magnification field captures.
    \item \textbf{Pre-Extracted Deep Representations and Zero-Barrier Machine Learning Readiness}: The repository provides pre-computed, $L_2$-normalized 768-dimensional feature vectors extracted from ConvNeXt-Tiny, allowing researchers to benchmark clustering, metric learning, and retrieval pipelines without high-performance GPU infrastructure.
    \item \textbf{Multimodal Vision-Language and Explainable Forensic AI Readiness}: Beyond standalone image classification, the repository provides an expert-curated table of structured macroscopic anatomical feature descriptors (\texttt{metadata/anatomical\_features.csv}) cataloging 14 diagnostic morphological axes (heartwood coloration, growth rings, pore porosity and grouping, vessel occlusion by tyloses and calcite/polyphenol deposits, aliform/confluent axial parenchyma configurations, storied rays, and physical density). This structured taxonomic ground truth enables emerging multimodal artificial intelligence paradigms, including Visual Question Answering (VQA) for timber anatomy (e.g., verifying diagnostic vessel-parenchyma combinations), semantic attribute-guided zero-shot species discrimination, cross-modal text-image retrieval, and explainable AI (XAI) validation ensuring deep neural saliency maps attend to legitimate anatomical structures rather than background artifacts.
    \item \textbf{Dual Empirical Benchmarking Testbed}: Beyond image curation, the dataset includes reproducible baselines for both supervised fine-grained classification under natural class imbalance (ConvNeXt-Tiny with Focal Loss, top-1 accuracy $90.42\%$) and deep metric representation learning (Semi-Hard Triplet Loss, Recall@1 $99.92\%$).
    \item \textbf{Unique Forensic Positioning Against Existing Public Collections}: Unlike prior macroscopic wood collections that exhibit low CITES coverage ($<15\%$), lower optical magnifications ($10\times$), or unmonitored random image splits with pervasive specimen leakage, IC4SDMacroWood uniquely bridges high optical resolution, forensic CITES concentration, governed split integrity, and open-access metadata auditability (Table~\ref{tab:dataset_comparison}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

Accurate identification of timber species is an essential regulatory and forensic requirement in global forest-product supply chains, particularly for high-value tropical hardwoods subject to international trade controls under CITES Appendix~II~\cite{dormontt2015,cites}. While conventional microtome sectioning and histological light microscopy remain definitive diagnostic standards, they require extensive laboratory processing: high-density tropical timbers (frequently exceeding $0.85\,\text{g/cm}^3$, such as \textit{Dalbergia} and \textit{Pterocarpus}) require prolonged thermal or chemical softening (often 12--24~hours), sliding microtome sectioning at $15\text{--}25\,\mu\text{m}$, histological staining, permanent slide mounting, and exhaustive cross-referencing against microscopic IAWA anatomical feature lists~\cite{iawa1989,wiedenhoeft2011}. In institutional forensic practice, this multi-step diagnostic workflow typically requires 2 to 5 business days per sample and depends on specialized xylarium facilities and highly trained wood anatomists~\cite{dormontt2015}.

Consequently, automated macroscopic wood identification on transverse (end-grain) surfaces using optical macro-imaging and computer vision has emerged as an accessible, non-destructive screening modality~\cite{woodreview,ravindran2020,ravindran2022}. Under this macroscopic approach, mechanical surface surfacing and progressive grit polishing (P120--P600) require only a few minutes, followed by standardized optical capture ($50\times$, $12\,\mu\text{m/px}$) and automated deep learning inference. This provides front-line regulatory officers and wood anatomists with rapid, accessible decision support to triage suspicious timber consignments at transit hubs prior to commissioning exhaustive laboratory histology.

Deep learning approaches to timber authentication increasingly depend on public reference datasets; however, the practical validity and reproducibility of reported models rely critically on data curation quality, taxonomic verification, and partition governance. In macroscopic timber collections, fine-grained anatomical resemblances among congeneric sister taxa~\cite{liu2025,song2025} and natural class imbalances pose significant operational challenges. A critical operational reality in reference xylaria is the severe scarcity of physical wood blocks for rare and legally regulated timber species ($|\mathcal{G}_c| \le 10$ physical specimens per taxon, with acute bottlenecks such as only 1 specimen for \textit{Dalbergia cochinchinensis}, 2 for \textit{Guibourtia coleosperma}, and 4 for \textit{Afzelia africana} and \textit{Sindora cochinchinensis}, as documented in Table~\ref{tab:taxonomic_inventory}). Under such small-sample regimes, enforcing dogmatic three-way whole-block isolation across training, validation, and test subsets mathematically deprives minority classes of evaluation captures, triggering severe class starvation ($\text{CCR} < 100\%$) and precluding empirical verification for the most critical forensic taxa. Conversely, unconstrained random image splitting causes pervasive, unmeasured specimen leakage that distorts held-out evaluations. To address this operational dilemma, IC4SDMacroWood introduces an authenticated macroscopic image dataset governed by a specimen-aware partition protocol: by formally modeling specimen allocation as an audited multi-objective trade-off, the release guarantees a 100\% Class Coverage Rate across all 19 taxa while strictly auditing and bounding specimen boundary sharing ($\text{SLR} = 30.6\%$) and guaranteeing absolute bitwise deduplication ($\text{SHA-256 Cross-Split Overlap} = 0$), accompanied by structured metadata, pre-computed deep feature representations, and technical-validation baselines for reproducible forensic wood identification.

\subsection{Partition Allocations and Class Distributions}

The 6,414 captures are partitioned following a governed specimen-aware allocation protocol across physical wood blocks (Training: 3,959 images, $61.72\%$; Validation: 1,265 images, $19.72\%$; Test: 1,190 images, $18.55\%$). Crucially, all 19 species achieve a 100\% Class Coverage Rate ($\text{CCR} = 100.0\%$, 19/19 taxa) across all three partitions.

In authentic timber reference archives characterized by small specimen cohorts ($|\mathcal{G}_c| \le 10$ physical specimens across all 19 taxa, totaling 148 verified wood blocks, with acute bottlenecks such as only 1 physical specimen for \textit{Dalbergia cochinchinensis}, 2 for \textit{Guibourtia coleosperma}, and 4 for \textit{Afzelia africana} and \textit{Sindora cochinchinensis}), dogmatic three-way whole-block isolation inevitably deprives minority CITES taxa of evaluation captures. Under such small-cohort constraints, enforcing complete block isolation on a single-specimen class like \textit{Dalbergia cochinchinensis} mathematically renders validation or test coverage impossible ($\text{CCR} < 100\%$). To avert minority-class starvation while maintaining rigorous evaluation standards, specimen-level allocation adopts an empirical Pareto trade-off that maintains controlled boundary sharing across partitions (Train--Val: 21 specimens; Train--Test: 12 specimens; Val--Test: 12 specimens; overall Specimen Leakage Rate $\text{SLR} = 30.6\%$). Crucially, to prevent data contamination, exact duplicate captures across split boundaries were systematically audited and eliminated: 256-bit SHA-256 cryptographic hashing verifies zero bitwise image duplication between partitions ($\text{SHA-256 Cross-Split Overlap} = 0$), ensuring that every evaluation capture represents an authentic, distinct optical field of view. Figure~\ref{fig:eda_split} illustrates per-species image volumes across partitions, and Table~\ref{tab:split_allocation} provides the comprehensive specimen and image allocation manifest across all 19 taxa.

\subsection{Diagnostic Macroscopic Anatomical Features and Multimodal Descriptors}
\label{sec:anatomical_features}

Macroscopic timber identification on transverse end-grain surfaces provides an effective diagnostic screening modality because it simultaneously reveals all primary secondary xylem tissue systems~\cite{wiedenhoeft2011}. Imaged at standardized $50\times$ optical magnification ($12\,\mu\text{m}$ spatial resolution), taxonomic differentiation across the 19 Fabaceae species is governed by four primary morphological axes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Vascular Porosity and Lumen Occlusion}: All 19 taxa exhibit diffuse-porous wood (with occasional semi-ring-porous tendencies in \textit{Dalbergia rimosa}), with vessel apertures predominantly solitary or in short radial multiples. Vessel tangential diameters range from fine pores ($90$--$160\,\mu\text{m}$ in \textit{Guibourtia} and \textit{Dalbergia melanoxylon}) to large pores ($180$--$260\,\mu\text{m}$ in \textit{Afzelia}). In heartwood, vessels in \textit{Dalbergia} and \textit{Pterocarpus} are frequently occluded by dark organic polyphenolic deposits, whereas lumina in \textit{Afzelia} and \textit{Sindora} contain conspicuous white crystalline deposits.
    \item \textbf{Axial Parenchyma Topography}: Axial parenchyma configuration serves as the primary taxonomic discriminator within the Leguminosae family: (i) prominent winged lozenge-aliform to confluent paratracheal sheaths forming pale halos in \textit{Afzelia}; (ii) fine, wavy concentric tangential bands (1--3 cells wide) forming reticulate patterns in \textit{Dalbergia} and \textit{Pterocarpus}; and (iii) regular continuous marginal bands demarcating growth increments in \textit{Sindora} and \textit{Guibourtia}.
    \item \textbf{Axial Intercellular Secretory Canals}: Regularly spaced axial resin canals embedded strictly within continuous marginal parenchyma bands uniquely isolate the genus \textit{Sindora} from all other examined taxa, precluding cross-genus misidentification with rosewoods or padauks.
    \item \textbf{Heartwood Chemomorphology and Coloration}: Secondary extractive deposition yields diagnostic optical contrast, including the intense purple hues of photo-oxidized peltogynoids in \textit{Peltogyne}, dark vertical pigment striping in \textit{Dalbergia} and \textit{Guibourtia ehie}, and vibrant orange-red extractives in \textit{Pterocarpus}.
\end{enumerate}

To enrich the benchmark beyond single-label classification and facilitate emerging multimodal artificial intelligence research, the dataset incorporates an expert-curated table of structured macroscopic anatomical descriptors (\texttt{metadata/anatomical\_features.csv}). \section{Experimental Design, Materials and Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Specimen Preparation and Optical Macro-Imaging}

Specimen preparation and optical surface conditioning followed standardized multi-stage laboratory procedures:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Orthogonal Transverse Surfacing}: End-grain transverse surfaces were planed strictly perpendicular to the longitudinal stem axis using an industrial sliding table saw fitted with a carbide circular blade.
    \item \textbf{Progressive Polishing}: Surfaces were smoothed sequentially on a motorized rotary platen using silicon-carbide abrasives across four calibrated grit gradations: P120, P240, P400, and P600.
    \item \textbf{Vascular Lumen De-Dusting}: Microscopic sawdust residues lodged within vessel apertures were purged using directed dry compressed-air jets at $6\,\text{bar}$ pressure, ensuring clear visibility of pore lumens and parenchyma sheaths.
    \item \textbf{Calibrated Optical Macro-Imaging}: Images were captured at $50\times$ optical magnification using a specialized digital macro-imaging sensor system ($f/8.0$, fixed working distance $15\,\text{cm}$, exposure $1/60\,\text{s}$, ISO 100) delivering an optical spatial resolution of $12\,\mu\text{m}$ per pixel. Illumination was provided by a shadowless, daylight-balanced ($5600\,\text{K}$) diffuse circular LED ring light ($4500\,\text{lux}$ uniform illuminance). White balance was calibrated using a standard color card.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Independent Taxonomic Label Verification and Inter-Rater Reliability}
\label{sec:inter_rater}

To establish rigorous ground-truth authenticity for forensic customs enforcement and timber trade compliance, all physical wood specimens ($|\mathcal{G}_c| = 148$ wood blocks) and their corresponding macroscopic captures were validated through an independent, double-blind verification protocol conducted by two certified forestry wood anatomists. Both experts independently evaluated each specimen and transverse capture against diagnostic wood anatomy keys established by the International Association of Wood Anatomists (IAWA)~\cite{iawa1989,wiedenhoeft2011}, cross-examining vascular porosity, axial parenchyma topography (aliform wings, confluent bands, and marginal lines), ray characteristics, and secondary heartwood chemomorphology.

Inter-rater reliability between the two independent experts was quantitatively audited using both raw observed percentage agreement ($P_o$) and Cohen's kappa coefficient ($\kappa$)~\cite{cohen1960}:
\begin{equation}
\kappa = \frac{P_o - P_e}{1 - P_e},
\end{equation}
where $P_o$ denotes the observed proportional concordance across all 6,414 captures, and $P_e$ represents the expected chance agreement under marginal class distributions. Across the initial blind evaluation phase, the two experts achieved an observed concordance of $P_o = 98.65\%$ (6,327 of 6,414 captures identically identified at the species level) and a Cohen's kappa of $\kappa = 0.985$ (95\% CI: $[0.980, 0.990]$), denoting near-perfect diagnostic agreement. Initial ambiguities ($1.35\%$, 87 captures) were localized exclusively between congeneric sister taxa with pronounced anatomical convergence (specifically between \textit{Afzelia pachyloba} and \textit{Afzelia bella}). Disputed specimens were subsequently resolved through a joint adjudication panel with reference herbarium vouchers, supplemented by air-dry density verification and long-wave ultraviolet (UV $365\,\text{nm}$) fluorescence testing on freshly planed surfaces, establishing 100\% unanimous consensus ground-truth labeling prior to dataset release.

\subsection{Quality Control and Governed Specimen-Aware Partitioning}

To guarantee benchmark integrity, a rigorous two-phase quality control and governed partitioning pipeline was executed:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Focus Sharpness Filtering and Laplacian Quantification}: Across initial imaging campaigns, a total of 6,680 raw macroscopic cross-sectional captures were recorded. Optical focus sharpness was evaluated quantitatively using the variance of the discrete Laplacian operator on grayscale channels:
    \begin{equation}
    \sigma^2_{\text{Laplacian}} = \frac{1}{H \times W} \sum_{x=1}^H \sum_{y=1}^W \left( \nabla^2 I(x,y) - \mu_{\nabla^2} \right)^2.
    \end{equation}
    Captures exhibiting $\sigma^2_{\text{Laplacian}} < 100$ were flagged as unfocused, micro-blurred, or motion-degraded and systematically purged from the repository. This objective sharpness threshold eliminated exactly 266 blurred captures ($3.98\%$ of raw acquisitions), yielding a curated cohort of exactly 6,414 high-quality standardized captures ($\sigma^2_{\text{Laplacian}} \ge 100$, documented per image in \texttt{metadata.csv}).
    \item \textbf{Bitwise Duplicate Auditing via SHA-256 Hashing}: To ensure zero data redundancy and prevent duplicate captures across subsets, cryptographic 256-bit hashes were computed for every image using Python's standard \texttt{hashlib.sha256()} library. The complete cryptographic audit is packaged in \texttt{metadata/release\_manifest.csv}, documenting each file's canonical identifier (\texttt{image\_id}), relative file path (\texttt{relative\_path}), file size in bytes (\texttt{file\_size\_bytes}), partition membership (\texttt{split}), and 64-character hexadecimal SHA-256 digest (\texttt{sha256}). Pairwise set intersections of hash sets across partitions ($\mathcal{H}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\text{val}} = \emptyset$, $\mathcal{H}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset$, and $\mathcal{H}_{\text{val}} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset$) confirmed zero bitwise cross-split duplication ($\text{SHA-256 Overlap} = 0$), mathematically verifying that every image in the validation and test sets constitutes an authentic, distinct optical capture.
    \item \textbf{Governed Benchmark Partitioning}: To ensure realistic field generalization while avoiding minority-class starvation, images were partitioned into Train ($N=3,959$), Validation ($N=1,265$), and Test ($N=1,190$) subsets, maintaining a 100\% Class Coverage Rate across all 19 taxa with a controlled specimen boundary sharing rate of $\text{SLR} = 30.6\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:split_allocation}).
\end{enumerate}

Standardized $224 \times 224$ px images are normalized during training using ImageNet channel statistics ($\boldsymbol{\mu} = [0.485, 0.456, 0.406]$, $\boldsymbol{\sigma} = [0.229, 0.224, 0.225]$). Training augmentations include random resized crops (scale $0.8$--$1.0$), random horizontal and vertical flips ($p = 0.5$), mild color jitter (factors of $0.25$, hue $0.05$), and subtle grayscale conversion ($p = 0.05$).

These experiments demonstrate end-to-end dataset execution and provide reproducible reference baselines for future studies rather than an exhaustive algorithmic benchmark.

\subsubsection{Deep Metric Representation Learning Baseline}
\label{sec:metric_baseline}

In addition to supervised classification, a deep metric representation baseline was evaluated using \textbf{Semi-Hard Triplet Loss} to provide reference embedding-space quality metrics and nearest-neighbor retrieval verification. To prevent ambiguity between distributed data assets and technical-validation modeling, we explicitly distinguish the dimensionality of these representations:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Distributed General-Purpose Embeddings ($768$-d)}: The publicly archived asset \texttt{embeddings/convnext\_tiny.npy} contains general-purpose, $L_2$-normalized 768-dimensional feature representations pooled directly from the frozen ConvNeXt-Tiny backbone pre-trained on ImageNet-1K. These static embeddings are distributed to facilitate rapid downstream experimentation (e.g., linear probing, clustering, zero-shot exploration) without requiring GPU compute.
    \item \textbf{Metric Learning Baseline Head ($256$-d)}: For deep metric learning validation, the ConvNeXt-Tiny backbone was coupled with a dedicated linear projection head ($768 \to 256$, with batch normalization). The resulting 256-dimensional vectors were $L_2$-normalized onto the unit hypersphere $\mathcal{S}^{255}$ ($d=256$) and fine-tuned under Semi-Hard Triplet Loss. This projection head represents an experimental validation model trained strictly to quantify geometric cluster compaction and open-set retrieval capability (Table~\ref{tab:metric_learning_comparison}) and is not bundled as a static file in the public data release.
\end{itemize}

Given anchor ($\boldsymbol{z}_a$), positive ($\boldsymbol{z}_p$), and negative ($\boldsymbol{z}_n$) projected embeddings on $\mathcal{S}^{255}$, the metric objective enforces:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{Triplet}} = \max\left(0, \|\boldsymbol{z}_a - \boldsymbol{z}_p\|_2^2 - \|\boldsymbol{z}_a - \boldsymbol{z}_n\|_2^2 + \alpha\right),
\end{equation}
with Euclidean margin $\alpha = 0.50$ on $L_2$-normalized embeddings ($\|\boldsymbol{z}\|_2 = 1$). Optimization employed online semi-hard negative mining~\cite{facenet} via a PK-sampler ($P=19$ classes, $K=4$ samples/class, batch size 76).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Several practical limitations and methodological trade-offs should be considered when utilizing the IC4SDMacroWood benchmark:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Modest Physical Specimen Cohort Sizes}: Physical reference cohorts remain modest ($|\mathcal{G}_c| \le 10$ wood blocks per species, aggregating 148 verified physical specimens across the 19 Fabaceae taxa), with acute scarcity bottlenecks for critically endangered or strictly protected timbers (specifically only 1 physical specimen for \textit{Dalbergia cochinchinensis} and 2 for \textit{Guibourtia coleosperma}). Consequently, intra-specific anatomical variation arising from geographic provenance, tree age, and micro-climatic growth conditions is not exhaustively sampled.
    \item \textbf{Controlled Specimen Allocation vs. Absolute Disjointness}: The canonical benchmark guarantees a 100\% Class Coverage Rate ($\text{CCR} = 100.0\%$) across all partitions via an audited Pareto trade-off that incurs a controlled specimen boundary sharing rate of $\text{SLR} = 30.6\%$. While exact bitwise duplicate captures across split boundaries are strictly purged ($\text{SHA-256 Overlap} = 0$), this partition is a controlled compromise designed to prevent minority-class starvation rather than an ideal, completely specimen-disjoint split. Downstream practitioners evaluating models for out-of-distribution deployment on entirely unseen physical logs should take this governed boundary sharing into account.
    \item \textbf{Laboratory Surface Preparation vs. In-the-Field Generalization}: All benchmark captures were recorded on orthogonally planed and progressively polished cross-sections (P120--P600 abrasives) under calibrated daylight-balanced ($5600\,\text{K}$) diffuse circular LED lighting. Reported baseline classification accuracy ($90.42\%$) and metric retrieval ($99.92\%$) may not directly generalize to rough chainsaw cuts, weathered end-grain surfaces, sawdust-obscured vessels, or uncontrolled solar glare encountered during real-time inspections at port container terminals or log landings.
    \item \textbf{Single Anatomical Plane}: The current benchmark focuses exclusively on the transverse (cross-sectional end-grain) plane. While the transverse view exposes primary diagnostic xylotomical systems (vessels, axial parenchyma halos, and growth increments), incorporating longitudinal radial and tangential surfaces~\cite{rosadasilva2022} in future releases could provide complementary discriminatory features for challenging sister species with convergent cross-sectional anatomy.
\end{itemize}
