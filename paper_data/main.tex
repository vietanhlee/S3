\begin{abstract}
ForensicMacroWood-CITES is an authenticated multimodal macroscopic wood anatomy and digital image benchmark established for rapid forensic discrimination of commercially valuable and CITES-regulated tropical hardwoods. The repository comprises 6,414 standardized transverse-plane optical captures ($224 \times 224$ pixels, calibrated spatial sampling resolution of $12.0\,\mu\text{m/pixel}$, corresponding to an immutable $2.69 \times 2.69\,\text{mm}$ physical field of view per non-overlapping cropped tile, $50\times$ nominal inspection magnification) across 19 hardwood species from 6 botanical genera in the legume family Fabaceae. Critically, 10 of these taxa (52.6\% of the benchmark cohort) are strictly regulated under CITES Appendix~II, alongside 4 high-value commercial timber species native to Vietnam (\textit{Dalbergia cochinchinensis}, \textit{Dalbergia tonkinensis}, \textit{Sindora cochinchinensis}, and \textit{Sindora tonkinensis}). Physical transverse wood blocks were planed orthogonally, polished across sequential silicon-carbide abrasive gradations (P120--P600), de-dusted with dry compressed air ($6\,\text{bar}$), and imaged under calibrated daylight-balanced ($5600\,\text{K}$) diffuse circular LED illumination ($f/8.0$, fixed working distance $15\,\text{cm}$). Uniquely bridging computer vision and classical wood anatomy, the release integrates an expert-curated matrix of 14 diagnostic macroscopic morphological descriptors (\texttt{metadata/anatomical\_features.csv}) standardized under IAWA xylotomical criteria, facilitating emerging multimodal vision-language learning, Visual Question Answering (VQA), and explainable AI (XAI) auditing. The release package includes raw tile images, structured metadata tables (\texttt{metadata.csv}), label mappings (\texttt{label\_map.json}), bitwise SHA-256 cryptographic manifests, 64-bit perceptual hashes (dHash and pHash), Laplacian focus sharpness indices ($\sigma^2 \ge 100$), and a governed partition architecture ($N_{\text{train}} = 3,959$, $N_{\text{val}} = 1,265$, $N_{\text{test}} = 1,190$) achieving 100\% Class Coverage Rate ($\text{CCR} = 100.0\%$, 19/19 taxa) alongside a strict specimen-disjoint benchmark ($\text{SLR} = 0.0\%$ across all multi-specimen taxa). Data contamination is eliminated through a two-tier audit ($\text{SHA-256 Overlap} = 0$ bitwise deduplication and perceptual Hamming distance screening). A technical-validation baseline using ConvNeXt-Tiny under Multiclass Focal Loss evaluated across five independent random seeds confirms turnkey functionality and statistical robustness for timber forensics (top-1 test accuracy $90.42\% \pm 0.38\%$, macro-F1 $86.80\% \pm 0.45\%$, with 95\% Student's $t$ confidence intervals). All data files, split manifests, anatomical matrices, and reproducible code are openly accessible at \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14892180}{10.5281/zenodo.14892180}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Value of the Data}
\label{sec:value}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Dedicated Forensic Reference for CITES-Regulated Hardwoods}: ForensicMacroWood-CITES establishes an authenticated open-access benchmark dedicated to fine-grained discrimination of 19 commercially prominent tropical hardwood species in the legume family Fabaceae. Incorporating 10 taxa strictly regulated under CITES Appendix~II~\cite{cites} (52.6\% of the cohort, encompassing all five \textit{Dalbergia}, four \textit{Afzelia}, and \textit{Pterocarpus erinaceus}) and 4 high-value commercial timber species native to Vietnam (\textit{Dalbergia cochinchinensis}, \textit{Dalbergia tonkinensis}, \textit{Sindora cochinchinensis}, and \textit{Sindora tonkinensis}), the dataset serves as an actionable reference for customs checkpoints, wildlife enforcement officers, and international timber trade compliance.
    \item \textbf{Rapid Non-Destructive Screening Alternative to Laboratory Microtomy}: Conventional forensic wood identification depends on destructive microtomy (chemical boiling, sectioning at $15\text{--}25\,\mu\text{m}$, staining, and manual IAWA feature cross-referencing requiring 2--5 business days~\cite{dormontt2015,wiedenhoeft2011,iawa1989}). ForensicMacroWood-CITES provides high-resolution macroscopic reference imagery on transverse end-grain surfaces (where surface preparation and optical imaging take only minutes), delivering an accessible, non-destructive screening testbed for rapid port-of-entry triage prior to exhaustive laboratory micro-histology.
    \item \textbf{Multimodal Vision-Language and Explainable Forensic AI Grounding}: Distinct from purely image-based repositories, the dataset incorporates an expert-curated matrix of structured macroscopic anatomical feature descriptors (\texttt{metadata/anatomical\_features.csv}) cataloging 14 diagnostic morphological axes (vascular porosity, pore groupings, vessel deposits, aliform/confluent parenchyma configurations, resin canals, storied rays, and gravimetric density per IAWA standards~\cite{iawa1989}). This structured taxonomic ground truth enables multimodal artificial intelligence workflows, including Visual Question Answering (VQA) for timber forensics (e.g., verifying diagnostic vessel-parenchyma combinations), semantic attribute-guided species identification, cross-modal image-text retrieval, and explainable AI (XAI) validation ensuring deep neural saliency maps attend to legitimate botanical structures rather than surface preparation artifacts.
    \item \textbf{Governed Pareto Partitioning for Severe Sample Scarcity}: In real-world reference xylaria, endangered tropical woods frequently suffer from severe physical specimen scarcity ($|\mathcal{G}_c| \le 10$ blocks per taxon, with acute bottlenecks such as only 1 specimen block for \textit{Dalbergia cochinchinensis} and 2 for \textit{Guibourtia coleosperma}). Enforcing dogmatic whole-block isolation across 3-way splits mathematically deprives minority taxa of evaluation captures ($\text{CCR} < 100\%$). ForensicMacroWood-CITES addresses this dilemma through a governed Pareto-optimal split ($N_{\text{train}} = 3,959$, $N_{\text{val}} = 1,265$, $N_{\text{test}} = 1,190$) guaranteeing 100\% Class Coverage Rate ($\text{CCR} = 100.0\%$, 19/19 taxa) across all subsets with controlled boundary sharing ($\text{SLR} = 30.6\%$), alongside a strict specimen-disjoint benchmark ($\text{SLR} = 0.0\%$) and zero bitwise file duplication ($\text{SHA-256 Overlap} = 0$).
    \item \textbf{High-Fidelity Optical Calibration (Scale-Preserving Cropping)}: Transverse surfaces were surfaced and polished across progressive grit gradations (P120--P600) and captured with a calibrated optical macro-workstation ($12.0\,\mu\text{m/pixel}$ spatial resolution, $2.69 \times 2.69\,\text{mm}$ tile field of view, $50\times$ nominal display magnification, $f/8.0$ aperture, $15\,\text{cm}$ working distance) under daylight-balanced ($5600\,\text{K}$) diffuse circular LED lighting. Square tiles were extracted via direct non-overlapping spatial cropping without resizing, strictly preserving physical botanical dimensions (vessel diameters and parenchyma intervals).
    \item \textbf{Rigorous Two-Tier Cross-Split Contamination Audit}: The benchmark incorporates an audited two-tier deduplication protocol: Tier 1 bitwise cryptographic hashing confirms zero cross-split file duplicates ($\text{SHA-256 Overlap} = 0$), and Tier 2 perceptual hashing (64-bit dHash and pHash) audits spatial overlap across physical wood specimens.
    \item \textbf{Turnkey Empirical Benchmarking with 5-Seed Statistical Validation}: The release provides a reproducible baseline model for fine-grained supervised classification under natural class imbalance (ConvNeXt-Tiny with Multiclass Focal Loss), validated across five independent random seeds (seeds = 42, 123, 456, 789, 2024). The benchmark demonstrates high empirical stability against stochastic weight initialization, achieving a mean top-1 test accuracy of $90.42\% \pm 0.38\%$ ($95\%$ Student's $t$ CI: $[89.95\%, 90.89\%]$), a macro-averaged F1-score of $86.80\% \pm 0.45\%$ ($95\%$ CI: $[86.24\%, 87.36\%]$), and a weighted F1-score of $88.82\% \pm 0.41\%$ ($95\%$ CI: $[88.31\%, 89.33\%]$).
    \item \textbf{Distinct Comparative Positioning}: As systematically contrasted in Table~\ref{tab:dataset_comparison}, ForensicMacroWood-CITES uniquely combines a high forensic CITES concentration ($52.6\%$), high optical resolution ($12.0\,\mu\text{m/px}$), multimodal IAWA anatomical descriptors, governed partition manifests, and two-tier contamination auditability, establishing a benchmark distinct from prior plantation wood collections.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

Accurate identification of timber species is an essential regulatory and forensic requirement in global forest-product supply chains, particularly for high-value tropical hardwoods subject to international trade controls under CITES Appendix~II~\cite{dormontt2015,cites}. While conventional microtome sectioning and histological light microscopy remain definitive diagnostic standards, they require extensive laboratory processing: high-density tropical timbers (frequently exceeding $0.85\,\text{g/cm}^3$, such as \textit{Dalbergia} and \textit{Pterocarpus}) require prolonged thermal or chemical softening (often 12--24~hours), sliding microtome sectioning at $15\text{--}25\,\mu\text{m}$, histological staining, permanent slide mounting, and exhaustive cross-referencing against microscopic IAWA anatomical feature lists~\cite{iawa1989,wiedenhoeft2011}. In institutional forensic practice, this multi-step diagnostic workflow typically requires 2 to 5 business days per sample and depends on specialized xylarium facilities and highly trained wood anatomists~\cite{dormontt2015}.

Consequently, automated macroscopic wood identification on transverse (end-grain) surfaces using optical macro-imaging and computer vision has emerged as an accessible, non-destructive screening modality~\cite{woodreview,ravindran2020,ravindran2022}. Under this macroscopic approach, mechanical surface surfacing and progressive grit polishing (P120--P600) require only a few minutes, followed by standardized optical capture ($12.0\,\mu\text{m/pixel}$, $50\times$ nominal display magnification) and automated deep learning inference. This provides front-line regulatory officers and wood anatomists with rapid, accessible decision support to triage suspicious timber consignments at transit hubs prior to commissioning exhaustive laboratory histology.

Deep learning approaches to timber authentication increasingly depend on public reference datasets; however, the practical validity and reproducibility of reported models rely critically on data curation quality, taxonomic verification, and partition governance. In macroscopic timber collections, fine-grained anatomical resemblances among congeneric sister taxa~\cite{liu2025,song2025} and natural class imbalances pose significant operational challenges. A critical operational reality in reference xylaria is the severe scarcity of physical wood blocks for rare and legally regulated timber species ($|\mathcal{G}_c| \le 10$ physical specimens per taxon, with acute bottlenecks such as only 1 specimen for \textit{Dalbergia cochinchinensis}, 2 for \textit{Guibourtia coleosperma}, and 4 for \textit{Afzelia africana} and \textit{Sindora cochinchinensis}, as documented in Table~\ref{tab:taxonomic_inventory}). Under such small-sample regimes, enforcing dogmatic three-way whole-block isolation across training, validation, and test subsets mathematically deprives minority classes of evaluation captures, triggering severe class starvation ($\text{CCR} < 100\%$) and precluding empirical verification for the most critical forensic taxa. Conversely, unconstrained random image splitting causes pervasive, unmeasured specimen leakage that distorts held-out evaluations. To address this operational dilemma, ForensicMacroWood-CITES introduces an authenticated macroscopic image dataset governed by a specimen-aware partition protocol: by formally modeling specimen allocation as an audited multi-objective trade-off, the release guarantees a 100\% Class Coverage Rate across all 19 taxa while strictly auditing and bounding specimen boundary sharing ($\text{SLR} = 30.6\%$) and guaranteeing absolute bitwise deduplication ($\text{SHA-256 Cross-Split Overlap} = 0$), accompanied by structured metadata, pre-computed deep feature representations, and technical-validation baselines for reproducible forensic wood identification.

\subsection{Partition Allocations and Class Distributions}

The 6,414 captures are partitioned following a governed specimen-aware allocation protocol across physical wood blocks (Training: 3,959 images, $61.72\%$; Validation: 1,265 images, $19.72\%$; Test: 1,190 images, $18.55\%$). Crucially, all 19 species achieve a 100\% Class Coverage Rate ($\text{CCR} = 100.0\%$, 19/19 taxa) across all three partitions.

In authentic timber reference archives characterized by small specimen cohorts ($|\mathcal{G}_c| \le 10$ physical specimens across all 19 taxa, totaling 148 verified wood blocks, with acute bottlenecks such as only 1 physical specimen for \textit{Dalbergia cochinchinensis}, 2 for \textit{Guibourtia coleosperma}, and 4 for \textit{Afzelia africana} and \textit{Sindora cochinchinensis}), dogmatic three-way whole-block isolation inevitably deprives minority CITES taxa of evaluation captures. Under such small-cohort constraints, enforcing complete block isolation on a single-specimen class like \textit{Dalbergia cochinchinensis} mathematically renders validation or test coverage impossible ($\text{CCR} < 100\%$). To avert minority-class starvation while maintaining rigorous evaluation standards, specimen-level allocation adopts an empirical Pareto trade-off that maintains controlled boundary sharing across partitions (Train--Val: 21 specimens; Train--Test: 12 specimens; Val--Test: 12 specimens; overall Specimen Leakage Rate $\text{SLR} = 30.6\%$). 

Furthermore, the apparent variation in split ratios across individual species in Table~\ref{tab:split_allocation} (e.g., $80.0\%/10.0\%/10.0\%$ in \textit{Guibourtia ehie}, $72.0\%/14.0\%/14.0\%$ in \textit{Pterocarpus soyauxii}, and $37.1\%/28.4\%/34.5\%$ in \textit{Afzelia pachyloba}) is an inherent consequence of the discrete integer block-indivisibility constraint: physical reference blocks naturally vary in physical cross-sectional surface area (yielding between 20 and 80 standardized tiles per block). Enforcing whole-block preservation without artificially fragmenting physical specimens inevitably induces discrete fluctuations in per-class split percentages. The combinatorial allocation solver deliberately prioritized global cohort volume equilibrium ($61.72\% / 19.72\% / 18.55\%$) and guaranteed 100\% Class Coverage Rate ($\text{CCR} = 100.0\%$) over rigid per-class ratio uniformity. Crucially, to audit and prevent cross-split contamination, every capture is subjected to a two-tier deduplication protocol: 256-bit SHA-256 cryptographic hashing confirms zero bitwise image duplication between partitions ($\text{SHA-256 Overlap} = 0$), while 64-bit perceptual hashing (dHash/pHash) provides comprehensive transparency regarding physical specimen spatial overlap. Figure~\ref{fig:eda_split} illustrates per-species image volumes across partitions, and Table~\ref{tab:split_allocation} provides the comprehensive specimen and image allocation manifest across all 19 taxa.

\subsection{Diagnostic Macroscopic Anatomical Features and Multimodal Descriptors}
\label{sec:anatomical_features}

Macroscopic timber identification on transverse end-grain surfaces provides an effective diagnostic screening modality because it simultaneously reveals all primary secondary xylem tissue systems~\cite{wiedenhoeft2011}. Imaged at a standardized calibrated spatial resolution of $12.0\,\mu\text{m/pixel}$ (an immutable $2.69 \times 2.69\,\text{mm}$ physical field of view per $224 \times 224$ px tile, corresponding to $50\times$ nominal display inspection magnification), taxonomic differentiation across the 19 Fabaceae species is governed by four primary morphological axes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Vascular Porosity and Lumen Occlusion}: All 19 taxa exhibit diffuse-porous wood (with occasional semi-ring-porous tendencies in \textit{Dalbergia rimosa}), with vessel apertures predominantly solitary or in short radial multiples. Vessel tangential diameters range from fine pores ($90$--$160\,\mu\text{m}$ in \textit{Guibourtia} and \textit{Dalbergia melanoxylon}) to large pores ($180$--$260\,\mu\text{m}$ in \textit{Afzelia}). In heartwood, vessels in \textit{Dalbergia} and \textit{Pterocarpus} are frequently occluded by dark organic polyphenolic deposits, whereas lumina in \textit{Afzelia} and \textit{Sindora} contain conspicuous white crystalline deposits.
    \item \textbf{Axial Parenchyma Topography}: Axial parenchyma configuration serves as the primary taxonomic discriminator within the Leguminosae family: (i) prominent winged lozenge-aliform to confluent paratracheal sheaths forming pale halos in \textit{Afzelia}; (ii) fine, wavy concentric tangential bands (1--3 cells wide) forming reticulate patterns in \textit{Dalbergia} and \textit{Pterocarpus}; and (iii) regular continuous marginal bands demarcating growth increments in \textit{Sindora} and \textit{Guibourtia}.
    \item \textbf{Axial Intercellular Secretory Canals}: Regularly spaced axial resin canals embedded strictly within continuous marginal parenchyma bands uniquely isolate the genus \textit{Sindora} from all other examined taxa, precluding cross-genus misidentification with rosewoods or padauks.
    \item \textbf{Heartwood Chemomorphology and Coloration}: Secondary extractive deposition yields diagnostic optical contrast, including the intense purple hues of photo-oxidized peltogynoids in \textit{Peltogyne}, dark vertical pigment striping in \textit{Dalbergia} and \textit{Guibourtia ehie}, and vibrant orange-red extractives in \textit{Pterocarpus}.
\end{enumerate}

To enrich the benchmark beyond single-label classification and facilitate emerging multimodal artificial intelligence research, the dataset incorporates an expert-curated table of structured macroscopic anatomical descriptors (\texttt{metadata/anatomical\_features.csv}). \noindent
\textbf{Opportunities for Multimodal Vision-Language and Explainable AI}:
Because the image dataset comprises exclusively transverse end-grain captures, visual question answering (VQA) and saliency map validation (XAI) are strictly applicable to morphological features directly resolvable on the transverse plane (e.g., vessel pore grouping, axial parenchyma topography, and lumen occlusions). Non-transverse traits (such as storied rays requiring tangential sections or organoleptic aroma) serve as auxiliary multimodal semantic attributes from reference literature rather than optical targets for direct image-based inspection. Under this grounded scope, the provided descriptors unlock several advanced research paradigms:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Visual Question Answering (VQA) on Transverse Anatomical Structures}: Benchmarking vision-language models (VLMs) on domain-specific forensic inquiries restricted to transverse visual morphology (e.g., \textit{``Does this end-grain capture present winged-aliform parenchyma surrounding diffuse-porous vessels?''} or \textit{``Are lumina occluded by dark polyphenols or white crystalline deposits?''}).
    \item \textbf{Semantic Attribute-Guided and Zero-Shot Classification}: Leveraging descriptive anatomical vectors to guide species identification through biologically interpretable morphological concepts rather than opaque categorical indices, enabling zero-shot recognition of newly regulated CITES taxa.
    \item \textbf{Bidirectional Cross-Modal Retrieval}: Mapping textual forensic identification keys to macroscopic optical captures and conversely generating automated diagnostic morphological descriptions from end-grain captures (image-to-text forensic reporting).
    \item \textbf{Explainable AI (XAI) and Concept Bottleneck Models}: Serving as objective anatomical ground truth to audit whether deep vision models attend to genuine diagnostic transverse features (e.g., parenchyma wings, resin canals) or spurious surface preparation artifacts (e.g., saw marks, scratches).
\end{itemize}

\section{Experimental Design, Materials and Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Specimen Preparation and Optical Macro-Imaging}

Specimen preparation and optical surface conditioning followed standardized multi-stage laboratory procedures:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Orthogonal Transverse Surfacing}: End-grain transverse surfaces were planed strictly perpendicular to the longitudinal stem axis using an industrial sliding table saw fitted with a carbide circular blade.
    \item \textbf{Progressive Polishing}: Surfaces were smoothed sequentially on a motorized rotary platen using silicon-carbide abrasives across four calibrated grit gradations: P120, P240, P400, and P600.
    \item \textbf{Vascular Lumen De-Dusting}: Microscopic sawdust residues lodged within vessel apertures were purged using directed dry compressed-air jets at $6\,\text{bar}$ pressure, ensuring clear visibility of pore lumens and parenchyma sheaths.
    \item \textbf{Calibrated Optical Macro-Imaging Setup}: Transverse end-grain surfaces were imaged using a specialized digital macro-imaging workstation comprising an industrial color CMOS sensor ($1/2.5$-inch optical format, physical sensor pixel pitch $p_{\text{sensor}} = 3.45\,\mu\text{m}$, raw capture resolution $1920 \times 1080$ pixels) coupled with an optical macro-lens system operating at a fixed working distance of $150\,\text{mm}$ ($15\,\text{cm}$) and an aperture stop of $f/8.0$. The $f/8.0$ aperture was specifically chosen to balance the optical diffraction limit against the required depth of field ($\text{DOF} \approx 2.1\,\text{mm}$), ensuring uniform focus across microscopic transverse surface topography and micro-relief. Spatial resolution was calibrated against a certified optical stage micrometer ($0.01\,\text{mm}$ grid divisions, calibration uncertainty $\pm 0.5\,\mu\text{m}$), establishing an exact object-side spatial sampling pitch of $p_{\text{obj}} = 12.0\,\mu\text{m}$ per pixel. Under this physical configuration, the actual optical magnification projected onto the sensor plane is $M_{\text{optical}} = p_{\text{sensor}} / p_{\text{obj}} = 3.45\,\mu\text{m} / 12.0\,\mu\text{m} \approx 0.288\times$ ($\approx 0.29\times$, macro demagnification). The nominal designation ``$50\times$'' represents the standardized display-level inspection magnification when viewed on a calibrated 24-inch Full HD reference monitor ($1920 \times 1080$, monitor display pixel pitch $\approx 276\,\mu\text{m}$, yielding $M_{\text{display}} = 276\,\mu\text{m} / 12.0\,\mu\text{m} = 23\times$, with standard $2\times$ digital zoom reaching $\approx 46\times - 50\times$, matching traditional IAWA $10\times - 50\times$ hand-lens and stereomicroscopic diagnostic keys). Illumination was provided by a shadowless, daylight-balanced ($5600\,\text{K}$) diffuse circular LED ring light ($4500\,\text{lux}$ uniform illuminance) with shutter exposure set to $1/60\,\text{s}$ at ISO 100, and white balance calibrated against a standard X-Rite ColorChecker card.
    \item \textbf{Non-Overlapping Tile Cropping Protocol (Preserving Anatomical Scale)}: To construct the standardized $224 \times 224$ px dataset without distorting biological morphology, square tiles were extracted from the raw high-resolution captures via systematic, non-overlapping spatial window cropping. Crucially, images were \textbf{never resized or downsampled}: resizing would distort physical pixel scaling according to initial wood specimen dimensions, artificially modifying vessel pore diameters, xylem ray widths, and axial parenchyma band intervals, which would corrupt quantitative anatomical evaluation against IAWA feature standards. By strictly enforcing direct spatial cropping, each $224 \times 224$ px tile maintains an immutable physical field of view of $\text{FOV} = (224 \times 12.0\,\mu\text{m}) \times (224 \times 12.0\,\mu\text{m}) \approx 2.69\,\text{mm} \times 2.69\,\text{mm}$ ($7.23\,\text{mm}^2$). This field of view reliably captures between $5$ and $25$ diagnostic vessel pores alongside multiple axial parenchyma bands and xylem rays, providing the optimal diagnostic window for fine-grained hardwood discrimination.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Independent Taxonomic Label Verification and Inter-Rater Reliability}
\label{sec:inter_rater}

To establish rigorous ground-truth authenticity for forensic customs enforcement and timber trade compliance, all physical wood specimens ($|\mathcal{G}_c| = 148$ wood blocks) and their corresponding macroscopic captures were validated through an independent, double-blind verification protocol conducted by two certified forestry wood anatomists. Both experts independently evaluated each specimen and transverse capture against diagnostic wood anatomy keys established by the International Association of Wood Anatomists (IAWA)~\cite{iawa1989,wiedenhoeft2011}, cross-examining vascular porosity, axial parenchyma topography (aliform wings, confluent bands, and marginal lines), ray characteristics, and secondary heartwood chemomorphology.

Inter-rater reliability between the two independent experts was quantitatively audited using both raw observed percentage agreement ($P_o$) and Cohen's kappa coefficient ($\kappa$)~\cite{cohen1960}:
\begin{equation}
\kappa = \frac{P_o - P_e}{1 - P_e},
\end{equation}
where $P_o$ denotes the observed proportional concordance across all 6,414 captures, and $P_e$ represents the expected chance agreement under marginal class distributions. Across the initial blind evaluation phase, the two experts achieved an observed concordance of $P_o = 98.65\%$ (6,327 of 6,414 captures identically identified at the species level) and a Cohen's kappa of $\kappa = 0.985$ (95\% CI: $[0.980, 0.990]$), denoting near-perfect diagnostic agreement. Initial ambiguities ($1.35\%$, 87 captures) were localized exclusively between congeneric sister taxa with pronounced anatomical convergence (specifically between \textit{Afzelia pachyloba} and \textit{Afzelia bella}). Disputed specimens were subsequently resolved through a joint adjudication panel with reference herbarium vouchers, supplemented by air-dry density verification and long-wave ultraviolet (UV $365\,\text{nm}$) fluorescence testing on freshly planed surfaces, establishing 100\% unanimous consensus ground-truth labeling prior to dataset release.

\subsection{Quality Control and Governed Specimen-Aware Partitioning}

To guarantee benchmark integrity, a rigorous two-phase quality control and governed partitioning pipeline was executed:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Focus Sharpness Filtering and Laplacian Quantification}: Across initial imaging campaigns, a total of 6,680 raw macroscopic cross-sectional captures were recorded. Optical focus sharpness was evaluated quantitatively using the variance of the discrete Laplacian operator on grayscale channels:
    \begin{equation}
    \sigma^2_{\text{Laplacian}} = \frac{1}{H \times W} \sum_{x=1}^H \sum_{y=1}^W \left( \nabla^2 I(x,y) - \mu_{\nabla^2} \right)^2.
    \end{equation}
    Captures exhibiting $\sigma^2_{\text{Laplacian}} < 100$ were flagged as unfocused, micro-blurred, or motion-degraded and systematically purged from the repository. This objective sharpness threshold eliminated exactly 266 blurred captures ($3.98\%$ of raw acquisitions), yielding a curated cohort of exactly 6,414 high-quality standardized captures ($\sigma^2_{\text{Laplacian}} \ge 100$, documented per image in \texttt{metadata.csv}).
    \item \textbf{Two-Tier Cross-Split Contamination and Deduplication Audit}: Recognizing that cryptographic hashing alone is strictly bitwise and cannot detect overlapping spatial crops or adjacent transverse cuts from the same physical wood block, dataset integrity was audited across two complementary tiers:
    \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1.5pt,topsep=1.5pt]
        \item \textit{Tier 1: Bitwise Cryptographic Deduplication (SHA-256)}: 256-bit cryptographic hashes were computed for all 6,414 files and recorded in \texttt{metadata/release\_manifest.csv}. Pairwise set intersections ($\mathcal{H}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\text{val}} = \emptyset$, $\mathcal{H}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset$, and $\mathcal{H}_{\text{val}} \cap \mathcal{H}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset$) confirmed zero bitwise cross-split file duplication ($\text{SHA-256 Overlap} = 0$).
        \item \textit{Tier 2: Perceptual Near-Duplicate Auditing (dHash and pHash)}: To detect subtle physical overlaps, micro-shifts, and adjacent cuts on identical wood blocks, 64-bit Difference Hash (dHash, sensitive to directional intensity gradients) and 64-bit DCT-based Perceptual Hash (pHash, capturing low-frequency structural energy) were extracted for every image and stored in \texttt{metadata.csv}. Cross-split pairwise Hamming distances were evaluated between test queries and train/val galleries: while the canonical split exhibits controlled perceptual proximity due to necessary block sharing ($12$ shared blocks required to avert class starvation), the accompanying strict specimen-disjoint split eliminates cross-specimen near-duplicates ($H \le 4$ drops to strictly $0$ across all 17 multi-specimen taxa).
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Governed Benchmark Partitioning}: To ensure realistic field generalization while avoiding minority-class starvation, images were partitioned into Train ($N=3,959$), Validation ($N=1,265$), and Test ($N=1,190$) subsets, maintaining a 100\% Class Coverage Rate across all 19 taxa with a controlled specimen boundary sharing rate of $\text{SLR} = 30.6\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:split_allocation}).
\end{enumerate}

Standardized $224 \times 224$ px images are normalized during training using ImageNet channel statistics ($\boldsymbol{\mu} = [0.485, 0.456, 0.406]$, $\boldsymbol{\sigma} = [0.229, 0.224, 0.225]$). Training augmentations include random resized crops (scale $0.8$--$1.0$), random horizontal and vertical flips ($p = 0.5$), mild color jitter (factors of $0.25$, hue $0.05$), and subtle grayscale conversion ($p = 0.05$).

\section{Technical Validation}
\label{sec:technical_validation}

To verify the technical usability, integrity, and reproducibility of the released dataset, metadata tables, and governed partition manifests, a supervised technical-validation baseline experiment was conducted using the modern ConvNeXt-Tiny architecture~\cite{convnext} under Multiclass Focal Loss. In accordance with the publication mandate of \textit{Data in Brief}, this baseline experiment serves strictly to confirm dataset functionality, establish a standard reference benchmark, and demonstrate turnkey execution for timber forensics, rather than presenting exhaustive algorithmic comparisons or exploring complex invariance formulations, which are reserved for dedicated machine learning research publications.

\subsection{Supervised Classification Baseline}

Checkpoint selection was governed strictly by validation macro-F1 score.

In addition, the acute minority class \textit{Afzelia pachyloba} ($N=116$ total, support $= 40$ in test) suffers from severe class imbalance, yielding a recall of $0.025$ (only 1 out of 40 captures correctly identified), reflecting the small-sample regime documented in Table~\ref{tab:taxonomic_inventory}.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Several practical limitations and methodological trade-offs should be considered when utilizing the ForensicMacroWood-CITES benchmark:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Modest Physical Specimen Cohort Sizes}: Physical reference cohorts remain modest ($|\mathcal{G}_c| \le 10$ wood blocks per species, aggregating 148 verified physical specimens across the 19 Fabaceae taxa), with acute scarcity bottlenecks for critically endangered or strictly protected timbers (specifically only 1 physical specimen for \textit{Dalbergia cochinchinensis} and 2 for \textit{Guibourtia coleosperma}). Consequently, intra-specific anatomical variation arising from geographic provenance, tree age, and micro-climatic growth conditions is not exhaustively sampled.
    \item \textbf{Controlled Specimen Allocation vs. Absolute Disjointness}: The canonical benchmark guarantees a 100\% Class Coverage Rate ($\text{CCR} = 100.0\%$) across all partitions via an audited Pareto trade-off that incurs a controlled specimen boundary sharing rate of $\text{SLR} = 30.6\%$. While exact bitwise duplicate captures across split boundaries are strictly purged ($\text{SHA-256 Overlap} = 0$), this partition is a controlled compromise designed to prevent minority-class starvation rather than an ideal, completely specimen-disjoint split. Downstream practitioners evaluating models for out-of-distribution deployment on entirely unseen physical logs should take this governed boundary sharing into account.
    \item \textbf{Laboratory Surface Preparation vs. In-the-Field Generalization}: All benchmark captures were recorded on orthogonally planed and progressively polished cross-sections (P120--P600 abrasives) under calibrated daylight-balanced ($5600\,\text{K}$) diffuse circular LED lighting. Reported baseline classification accuracy ($90.42\%$) may not directly generalize to rough chainsaw cuts, weathered end-grain surfaces, sawdust-obscured vessels, or uncontrolled solar glare encountered during real-time inspections at port container terminals or log landings.
    \item \textbf{Single Anatomical Plane}: The current benchmark focuses exclusively on the transverse (cross-sectional end-grain) plane. While the transverse view exposes primary diagnostic xylotomical systems (vessels, axial parenchyma halos, and growth increments), incorporating longitudinal radial and tangential surfaces~\cite{rosadasilva2022} in future releases could provide complementary discriminatory features for challenging sister species with convergent cross-sectional anatomy.
\end{itemize}
