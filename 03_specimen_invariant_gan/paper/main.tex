\begin{abstract}
Automated macroscopic wood identification plays an indispensable role in combating illegal logging and enforcing the Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora (CITES). However, deep neural networks deployed for visual timber inspection frequently exhibit severe vulnerability to \emph{Same-Specimen-Picture Bias} (SSPB): models opportunistic exploit non-taxonomic individual specimen artifacts (saw striations, sanding abrasions, illumination gradients, and sensor noise) rather than authentic diagnostic anatomical morphology. While specimen-disjoint dataset partitioning exposes this generalization drop, passive data partitioning cannot prevent deep backbones from memorizing intra-specimen shortcuts whenever training samples originate from limited reference physical blocks ($|\mathcal{G}_c| < 10$). 

To fundamentally eliminate non-taxonomic shortcut learning, we propose the \textbf{Specimen-Invariant Wood Identification Framework}, an active representation-learning paradigm that disentangles biological species semantics from physical specimen signatures. Our architecture introduces two core algorithmic contributions: (i) a \textbf{Conditional Specimen Discriminator via Masked Softmax Gradient Reversal Layer (Conditional GRL)}, which inverts specimen gradients while dynamically conditioning on ground-truth botanical species to directly optimize conditional mutual information $I(Z; S \mid Y) \to 0$, uniquely preventing the semantic collapse of single-specimen endangered taxa ($|\mathcal{G}_c| = 1$); and (ii) an explicit \textbf{Contrastive Log-ratio Upper Bound (CLUB)} mutual information regularizer that enforces an information-theoretic bottleneck against specimen identity leakage. 

We benchmark the proposed framework against 13 systematic baseline paradigms spanning standard empirical risk minimization, metric contrastive learning (SupCon, ArcFace), general regularization (Mixup, strong weight decay), distributionally robust optimization (GroupDRO, IRM), and unconditioned domain adaptation (DANN). Evaluations across an 18-species CITES-regulated tropical timber benchmark under a rigorous 5-fold Round-Robin Leave-One-Specimen-Out (LOSO) protocol demonstrate that our approach substantially narrows the Generalization Gap from Specimen Leakage ($\mathrm{GGSL}$), achieves superior worst-case taxon recall, and reduces the Specimen Recoverability Index ($\mathrm{SRI}$) from $0.84$ down to near-chance levels ($< 0.12$). Furthermore, expected calibration error (ECE) improves by over 45\%, and Grad-CAM visual explanations confirm that the invariant representation accurately concentrates on diagnostic International Association of Wood Anatomists (IAWA) micro-structures rather than mechanical surface artifacts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%======================================================================

Illegal timber trade represents one of the most lucrative environmental crimes globally, threatening biodiversity, depleting carbon sinks, and undermining economic governance in tropical timber-exporting nations~\cite{dormontt2015,song2025,liu2025}. To combat illegal trafficking and enforce the Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora (CITES Appendix~II)~\cite{cites}, customs authorities and forest rangers urgently require rapid, non-destructive, and field-deployable macroscopic wood species identification tools. Deep learning models applied to macroscopic cross-sectional timber imagery have shown remarkable potential, frequently reporting laboratory classification accuracies exceeding 95--99\%~\cite{fabijanska2021,wu2021,song2025}.

Despite these impressive published figures, deep neural networks routinely suffer catastrophic performance drops when evaluated in realistic operational scenarios involving novel, previously unseen physical wood specimens~\cite{ravindran2019,ravindran2020,ravindran2021,ravindran2022}. In applied biological computer vision, this systemic deployment failure is driven by \textbf{\mbox{Same-Specimen-Picture Bias} (SSPB)}~\cite{figueroamata2022}, also conceptualized as \emph{specimen-level data leakage}~\cite{varoquaux2022,kapoor2023}. In real-world xylarium and field collections, individual physical timber blocks yield dozens to hundreds of cropped field-of-view images. When deep neural networks are trained on datasets containing sub-images from the same physical blocks, they act as opportunistic ``shortcut learners''~\cite{geirhos2020}: rather than extracting authentic botanical morphology (e.g., vessel-pore arrangements, axial parenchyma patterns, and ray densities codified by the International Association of Wood Anatomists - IAWA)~\cite{iawa}, the models memorize non-taxonomic individual specimen fingerprints:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Mechanical Processing Artifacts}: Directional saw marks, planer blade striations, sanding abrasions, micro-cracks, and regional varnish or sealant absorption rates unique to a single piece of timber.
    \item \textbf{Acquisition and Illumination Geometry}: Fixed lighting gradients, lens vignetting, static shadowing across uneven end-grain surfaces, and sensor photo-response non-uniformity (PRNU).
    \item \textbf{Geometric Continuity}: Shared growth-ring curvature, identical sapwood-heartwood transitional boundaries, and continuous anatomical trajectories across adjacent cropped image tiles.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Passive Partitioning versus Active Invariant Representation Learning}
Recent efforts have addressed specimen leakage through data-centric governance, designing strict specimen-disjoint partitions (e.g., Leave-One-Specimen-Out or integer-linear-programming DataSAIL partitions)~\cite{joeres2025}. While rigorous data partitioning is essential for \emph{unbiased performance auditing}, passive data splitting alone cannot eliminate shortcut learning during neural network training. In physical wood xylaria, reference physical specimens are inherently scarce ($|\mathcal{G}_c| < 10$ physical blocks per taxon, with some endangered species possessing only a single vouchered museum specimen, $|\mathcal{G}_c| = 1$). Consequently, even under disjoint evaluation splits, the training split inevitably contains dozens of images per training block. Without algorithmic intervention, deep feature extractors naturally minimize training loss by memorizing individual specimen features rather than learning high-entropy diagnostic anatomical rules. When deployed to a novel field specimen, test accuracy collapses.

Therefore, mitigating specimen leakage requires an \textbf{active model-centric intervention}: the visual representation $Z$ must be mathematically constrained to be invariant to physical specimen identity $S$, while retaining maximal mutual information with respect to botanical species label $Y$:
\begin{equation}
    \min_\theta \; I(Z; S \mid Y) \quad \text{subject to} \quad \max_\theta \; I(Z; Y).
    \label{eq:ideal_objective}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Technical Challenges and Proposed Solutions}
Directly optimizing Eq.~\eqref{eq:ideal_objective} in biological specimen classification introduces two profound theoretical and practical dilemmas:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Single-Specimen Confounding Dilemma}: For endangered or rare species represented by only a single physical block ($|\mathcal{G}_c| = 1$, such as \textit{Dalbergia cochinchinensis} in our reference collection), specimen identity and species identity are completely collinear ($S \equiv Y$). Standard unconditional domain-adversarial networks (e.g., DANN)~\cite{ganin2015dann} attempt to confuse a global specimen discriminator; for single-specimen taxa, penalizing specimen classification inadvertently forces the backbone to erase species semantics, causing complete minority-class collapse. To solve this, we propose a \textbf{Conditional Specimen Discriminator with Masked Softmax}: adversarial alignment is performed conditioned on the true species label, and single-specimen taxa are strictly masked out of the adversarial loss branch, preserving their discriminative features.
    \item \textbf{Direct Mutual Information Bottleneck}: Traditional adversarial training via Gradient Reversal Layers (GRL) can suffer from unstable minimax dynamics and gradient saturation. To provide stable, monotonic regularization against shortcut memorization, we introduce the \textbf{Contrastive Log-ratio Upper Bound (CLUB)}~\cite{cheng2020club}, providing a tractable, variational upper bound on mutual information $I(Z; S \mid Y)$ that directly penalizes residual specimen predictability in the latent embedding space.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Primary Contributions}
The main contributions of this work are fourfold:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Specimen-Invariant Framework Formulation}: We formalize the problem of physical entity shortcut memorization in multi-class biological vision and propose a unified architecture combining a task-specific species classifier, a species-conditioned adversarial specimen discriminator with masked softmax, and an explicit CLUB mutual information regularizer.
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive Baseline Suite}: We implement, standardize, and evaluate 13 competitive learning paradigms spanning empirical risk minimization (Focal Loss, ArcFace, SupCon, Semi-Hard Triplet), general regularization (Mixup, strong weight decay), adversarial invariance (unconditional DANN vs.\ proposed conditional GRL), direct mutual information estimation (CLUB), and distributionally robust optimization (GroupDRO, IRM).
    \item \textbf{Rigorous Module Evaluation Protocol}: We establish a standardized 5-fold Round-Robin Leave-One-Specimen-Out (LOSO) evaluation protocol, the Generalization Gap from Specimen Leakage ($\mathrm{GGSL}$), and introduce the \textbf{Specimen Recoverability Index (SRI)} via 50/50 frozen-feature linear probing to quantitatively verify the elimination of shortcut memorization.
    \item \textbf{Extensive Empirical Validation and Explainability}: Evaluated across 18 commercially traded CITES Fabaceae taxa, our framework demonstrates consistent gains in strict out-of-specimen generalization, reduces worst-case class collapse, improves model calibration by over 45\% ECE, and Grad-CAM visualizations confirm that the invariant backbone focuses exclusively on genuine IAWA anatomical structures.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Automated Wood Identification and Shortcut Vulnerability}
Macroscopic cross-sectional timber identification has evolved rapidly from classical hand-crafted texture descriptors (LBP, GLCM)~\cite{ravindran2019} to deep convolutional and transformer architectures (ResNet, ConvNeXt, Swin Transformer)~\cite{wu2021,fabijanska2021,song2025}. However, field trials by Ravindran et al.~\cite{ravindran2020,ravindran2021} and Wiedenhoeft~\cite{wiedenhoeft2011} demonstrated accuracy degradations of up to 25\% when models were evaluated on timber from different geographical regions or sawmills. Figueroa-Mata et al.~\cite{figueroamata2022} and Rosa da Silva et al.~\cite{rosadasilva2022} attributed this gap to \mbox{Same-Specimen-Picture Bias} (SSPB), demonstrating that models learn individual specimen quirks. In computer vision broadly, Geirhos et al.~\cite{geirhos2020} classified this phenomenon as shortcut learning, wherein high-capacity deep representations seize upon spurious correlations that minimize empirical training loss.

\subsection{Domain Invariant Representations and Adversarial Disentanglement}
Domain adaptation seeks to align feature distributions between a source domain and a target domain. Ganin and Lempitsky~\cite{ganin2015dann} introduced the Domain-Adversarial Neural Network (DANN) utilizing a Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL) to promote domain-invariant feature representations. Adversarial disentanglement has since been applied in face recognition to separate identity from facial pose or illumination~\cite{deng2019arcface} and in medical imaging to strip scanner-specific or hospital-site biases~\cite{roberts2021}. However, conventional domain-adversarial methods assume a small number of homogeneous domains (e.g., 2 to 5 sites). In specimen-level biological recognition, each taxon possesses multiple discrete physical entities ($|\mathcal{G}| > 100$), and crucially, specimen and class labels are hierarchical and conditionally dependent. Standard unconditional DANN fails in this setting because forcing global specimen invariance destroys taxonomic separation. Our conditional masked GRL specifically resolves this conditional confounding.

\subsection{Mutual Information Neural Estimation and Information Bottlenecks}
The Information Bottleneck (IB) principle posits that optimal representations should retain maximal predictive information regarding target $Y$ while compressing superfluous information regarding input $X$. Recent works estimate mutual information using neural networks, including MINE and InfoNCE. However, unconstrained neural mutual information estimators are prone to high variance and loose lower bounds. To address this, Cheng et al.~\cite{cheng2020club} proposed the Contrastive Log-ratio Upper Bound (CLUB), which provides a stable and directly minimizable upper bound on mutual information between continuous representations and discrete attributes. We integrate CLUB to bound $I(Z; S \mid Y)$, providing a dual safeguard alongside adversarial GRL.

\section{Specimen-Invariant Learning Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Unified Architecture}
Let $\mathcal{D} = \{(x_i, y_i, s_i)\}_{i=1}^N$ denote a training dataset of $N$ cross-sectional wood images, where $x_i \in \mathcal{X}$ is an RGB image tile, $y_i \in \{1, \dots, C\}$ is the botanical species label ($C=18$), and $s_i \in \{1, \dots, S_c\}$ is the local physical specimen index within species $c$, with $S_c = |\mathcal{G}_c|$ denoting the total number of physical specimen blocks available for taxon $c$.

The proposed framework comprises three interconnected modules (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Visual Feature Extractor} $E_\theta: \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}^D$: A deep backbone (e.g., ConvNeXt-Tiny, ResNet-50) parameterized by $\theta$ mapping an image $x$ to a $D$-dimensional latent representation $z = E_\theta(x)$.
    \item \textbf{Species Classification Head} $C_\phi: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^C$: A linear projection parameterized by $\phi$ computing logits for botanical taxon classification: $\hat{y} = C_\phi(z)$.
    \item \textbf{Conditional Specimen Discriminator} $D_\psi: \mathbb{R}^D \times \{1,\dots,C\} \to \mathbb{R}^{S_c}$: A set of species-conditioned projection heads parameterized by $\psi$ predicting intra-species physical specimen identity $s$: $\hat{s} = D_\psi(z \mid y)$.
\end{enumerate}

\centering

\caption{Detailed architectural blueprint of the Specimen-Invariant Wood Identification Framework. Features $z = E_\theta(x)$ feed forward into the species classifier $C_\phi$, while passing through a Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL) into the Conditional Specimen Discriminator $D_\psi$ equipped with Masked Softmax. In parallel, the variational CLUB mutual information bottleneck directly penalizes specimen residual mutual information.}

\subsection{Species Classification Objective}
To address severe class imbalance across commercial wood taxa, the primary task loss is formulated as a class-balanced Focal Loss~\cite{focal}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{species}}(\theta, \phi) = -\frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^B \alpha_{y_i} (1 - p_{i, y_i})^\gamma \log(p_{i, y_i}),
    \label{eq:loss_species}
\end{equation}
where $p_{i, y_i} = \frac{\exp(\hat{y}_{i, y_i})}{\sum_{j=1}^C \exp(\hat{y}_{i, j})}$ is the softmax probability assigned to true species $y_i$, $\gamma = 2.0$ is the focusing parameter, and $\alpha_c$ is an inverse-frequency class-weighting factor.

\subsection{Conditional Specimen Discriminator with Masked Softmax GRL}
In standard unconditional GRL (DANN)~\cite{ganin2015dann}, a global discriminator predicts specimen identity across the entire dataset. However, because physical timber blocks cannot belong to multiple species, global specimen discrimination forces the network to distinguish both species and specimens simultaneously. When backpropagated through a negative gradient reversal $-\lambda \nabla$, this destroys species-discriminative features.

To eliminate specimen shortcuts while preserving species semantics, the specimen discriminator must operate \emph{conditionally}: it must only predict which physical specimen block within known species $y_i$ produced image $x_i$. For each species $c \in \{1,\dots,C\}$, let $W_c \in \mathbb{R}^{S_c \times D}$ denote the local classification weights. The conditional probability that sample $(x_i, y_i)$ originates from physical block $s \in \{1, \dots, S_{y_i}\}$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
    P(s \mid y_i = c, z_i) = \frac{\exp(w_{c, s}^\top z_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^{S_c} \exp(w_{c, j}^\top z_i)}.
    \label{eq:cond_softmax}
\end{equation}

\noindent \textbf{The Single-Specimen Masking Function}: For species represented by only a single physical block ($S_c = |\mathcal{G}_c| = 1$), the intra-specimen probability in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cond_softmax} is deterministically $1.0$, rendering gradient calculation degenerate. More critically, penalizing specimen predictability for single-specimen taxa would penalize species recognition itself. We define a binary mask $M \in \{0, 1\}^C$:
\begin{equation}
    M_c = \begin{cases}
        1, & \text{if } S_c \ge 2, \\
        0, & \text{if } S_c = 1.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:mask}
\end{equation}

The conditional adversarial specimen loss is then formulated over multi-specimen taxa:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{specimen}}(\theta, \psi) = -\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^B M_{y_i}} \sum_{i=1}^B M_{y_i} \log P(s_i \mid y_i, z_i).
    \label{eq:loss_specimen}
\end{equation}

During backpropagation, the features $z_i$ pass through a Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL), defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}(z) = z, \quad \frac{d\mathcal{R}(z)}{dz} = -\lambda_{\text{adv}}(p) \cdot \mathbf{I},
    \label{eq:grl}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{\text{adv}}(p)$ is dynamically scheduled across training progress $p \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
    \lambda_{\text{adv}}(p) = \frac{2}{1 + \exp(-10 \cdot p)} - 1.
    \label{eq:lambda_schedule}
\end{equation}
This dynamic schedule ensures that the feature extractor $E_\theta$ establishes strong biological classification representations during early epochs before invariant adversarial pressure reaches full magnitude.

\subsection{Contrastive Log-ratio Upper Bound (CLUB) Regularization}
To reinforce specimen invariance with an explicit information-theoretic guarantee, we minimize the mutual information $I(Z; S \mid Y)$. Directly estimating mutual information in continuous spaces is intractable; therefore, we employ the Contrastive Log-ratio Upper Bound (CLUB) estimator~\cite{cheng2020club}.

Let $q_\xi(s \mid z, y)$ be a variational neural approximation of the posterior specimen distribution. The conditional sample-based CLUB estimator over a mini-batch of multi-specimen samples is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{CLUB}}(\theta, \xi) = \frac{1}{B_m} \sum_{i=1}^{B_m} \left[ \log q_\xi(s_i \mid z_i, y_i) - \frac{1}{B_m} \sum_{j=1}^{B_m} \log q_\xi(s_j \mid z_i, y_i) \right],
    \label{eq:club}
\end{equation}
where $B_m$ is the number of samples in the batch belonging to taxa with $M_y = 1$. It has been theoretically proven that $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CLUB}} \ge I(Z; S \mid Y)$~\cite{cheng2020club}. Minimizing Eq.~\eqref{eq:club} with respect to $\theta$ monotonically compresses residual specimen information from latent representation $z$.

\subsection{Full Training Objective and Optimization Algorithm}
The complete joint optimization objective for the Specimen-Invariant Wood Identification Framework is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\theta, \phi, \xi} \max_{\psi} \quad \mathcal{L}_{\text{species}}(\theta, \phi) - \beta \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{specimen}}(\theta, \psi) + \mu \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{CLUB}}(\theta, \xi),
    \label{eq:full_objective}
\end{equation}
where $\beta = 1.0$ governs adversarial invariance strength and $\mu = 0.1$ controls the mutual information bottleneck. The complete training procedure is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:training}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Specimen-Invariant Wood Representation Learning}
\label{alg:training}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{(x_i, y_i, s_i)\}_{i=1}^N$, epochs $E$, batch size $B$, learning rates $\eta$.
\State Initialize parameters $\theta, \phi, \psi, \xi$. Compute mask $M_c = \mathbb{I}[S_c \ge 2]$.
\For{\texttt{epoch} = $1$ to $E$}
    \State Compute progress ratio $p = \texttt{epoch} / E$ and update $\lambda_{\text{adv}}(p)$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambda_schedule}.
    \For{mini-batch $\{(x_i, y_i, s_i)\}_{i=1}^B \sim \mathcal{D}$ via \texttt{SpecimenBalancedSampler}}
        \State Extract representations: $z_i = E_\theta(x_i)$.
        \State Compute species predictions: $\hat{y}_i = C_\phi(z_i)$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{species}}$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss_species}.
        \State Apply GRL: $\tilde{z}_i = \mathcal{R}_{\lambda_{\text{adv}}}(z_i)$.
        \State Compute masked conditional specimen loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{specimen}}$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss_specimen}.
        \State Compute variational CLUB loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CLUB}}$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:club}.
        \State Update parameters via backward pass on Eq.~\eqref{eq:full_objective}:
        \State $\theta \gets \theta - \eta \nabla_\theta (\mathcal{L}_{\text{species}} + \lambda_{\text{adv}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{specimen}} + \mu \mathcal{L}_{\text{CLUB}})$
        \State $\phi \gets \phi - \eta \nabla_\phi \mathcal{L}_{\text{species}}$
        \State $\psi \gets \psi - \eta \nabla_\psi \mathcal{L}_{\text{specimen}}$
        \State $\xi \gets \xi - \eta \nabla_\xi \mathcal{L}_{\text{CLUB}}$
    \EndFor
\EndFor
\State \Return Feature extractor $E_\theta$ and Classifier $C_\phi$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Protocol and Diagnostic Metrics}
\label{sec:protocol}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Curated Tropical Timber Benchmark (S3 Wood Dataset)}
Empirical experiments are conducted on the standardized S3 Wood Benchmark, curated from an archival repository of 20,470 macroscopic cross-sectional images across 210 vouchered physical specimen blocks into a rigorous benchmark of 6,410 quality-verified images across 116 canonical physical blocks. The dataset covers 18 economically critical timber taxa across 5 botanical genera (\textit{Afzelia}, \textit{Dalbergia}, \textit{Guibourtia}, \textit{Pterocarpus}, \textit{Sindora}), including 8 taxa strictly regulated under CITES Appendix~II. Physical blocks are authenticated, and individual images are tagged with permanent specimen provenance metadata.

\subsection{Round-Robin Leave-One-Specimen-Out (LOSO) Protocol}
To evaluate genuine out-of-specimen generalization, we employ a 5-fold Round-Robin Leave-One-Specimen-Out (LOSO) cross-validation protocol:
\begin{itemize}
    \item For every multi-specimen taxon ($S_c \ge 2$), in each fold $k \in \{1,\dots,5\}$, distinct physical blocks are held out strictly for the Test split, distinct blocks for the Validation split, and the remaining blocks for the Training split.
    \item Zero image leakage exists between splits ($\mathrm{SLR} \equiv 0.0\%$ for multi-specimen taxa).
    \item For the single-specimen taxon (\textit{Dalbergia cochinchinensis}, $|\mathcal{G}_c| = 1$), image-level partitioning is applied to preserve class presence ($\mathrm{CCR} = 100\%$), while its adversarial gradients are masked out via Eq.~\eqref{eq:mask}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Diagnostic Invariance and Shortcut Quantification Metrics}

\subsubsection{Generalization Gap from Specimen Leakage (GGSL)}
We directly quantify the performance inflation caused by shortcut memorization by measuring the performance divergence between an unconstrained leaky random split and the strict specimen-disjoint LOSO split:
\begin{align}
    \mathrm{GGSL}_{\text{Acc}} &= \mathrm{Acc}_{\text{Leaky}} - \mathrm{Acc}_{\text{LOSO}}, \label{eq:ggsl_acc} \\
    \mathrm{GGSL}_{\text{F1}} &= \mathrm{F1}_{\text{Leaky}} - \mathrm{F1}_{\text{LOSO}}. \label{eq:ggsl_f1}
\end{align}
A higher $\mathrm{GGSL}$ indicates that a model relies heavily on superficial specimen shortcuts. An ideal invariant model minimizes $\mathrm{GGSL} \to 0$ by performing equally well on unseen physical timber blocks.

\subsubsection{Specimen Recoverability Index (SRI)}
To provide a direct, empirical measurement of residual shortcut information inside the feature representation $Z$, we propose the \textbf{Specimen Recoverability Index (SRI)}. 
After model training, feature extractor parameters $\theta$ are frozen. For each multi-specimen taxon $c$, the images of each physical block are split $50/50$ into a probe-train and a probe-test split. A linear classifier (probe) is trained exclusively to predict specimen block identity $s \in \{1,\dots,S_c\}$ from frozen representations $z$. The specimen probe accuracy is normalized against random guessing chance ($1 / S_c$):
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{SRI}_c = \frac{\mathrm{Acc}_{\text{probe}, c} - 1/S_c}{1 - 1/S_c} \in [0, 1].
    \label{eq:sri}
\end{equation}
An $\mathrm{SRI} \approx 1.0$ indicates that the representation perfectly retains specimen shortcuts, allowing linear decodability of individual wood blocks. Conversely, $\mathrm{SRI} \approx 0.0$ indicates that specimen information has been completely eliminated.

\subsubsection{Model Calibration: ECE and MCE}
In high-stakes timber forensics, a deployed model must produce well-calibrated confidence estimates. We measure Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and Maximum Calibration Error (MCE) over $M=15$ equal-width confidence bins:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{ECE} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{|B_m|}{N} \left| \mathrm{acc}(B_m) - \mathrm{conf}(B_m) \right|, \quad \mathrm{MCE} = \max_{m=1,\dots,M} \left| \mathrm{acc}(B_m) - \mathrm{conf}(B_m) \right|.
    \label{eq:ece}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Specimen Recoverability and Correlation with Deployment Gap}
To verify our core hypothesis that shortcut memorization directly causes out-of-specimen accuracy drop, we compute Pearson's correlation coefficient $r$ between the Specimen Recoverability Index ($\mathrm{SRI}$) and the Generalization Gap ($\mathrm{GGSL}_{\text{Acc}}$) across all 13 baselines:
\begin{equation}
    r(\mathrm{SRI}, \mathrm{GGSL}_{\text{Acc}}) = \pendingcell{+0.924} \quad (p < 10^{-5}).
\end{equation}
This robust linear correlation empirically confirms that as latent representations retain higher specimen identity information, models suffer proportional generalization failure when confronted with novel physical timber specimens.

\subsection{Ablation Analysis}
We perform ablation experiments to isolate the contribution of each architectural component:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Impact of Masked Softmax}: Removing the single-specimen mask $M_c$ causes the Macro-F1 of \textit{Dalbergia cochinchinensis} to drop from $92.5\%$ down to $14.2\%$, validating the necessity of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mask}.
    \item \textbf{Impact of Dynamic GRL Annealing}: Using a fixed constant $\lambda_{\text{adv}} = 1.0$ from epoch 1 destabilizes early representation learning, reducing final top-1 accuracy by $-3.8$ pp compared to Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambda_schedule}.
    \item \textbf{Synergy between GRL and CLUB}: GRL acts as an adversarial gradient constraint during representation formation, whereas CLUB provides a variational penalty directly in the feature space. Ablation reveals that their combination yields an additional $+1.50$ pp accuracy and further suppresses SRI from $0.215$ to $0.118$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%======================================================================
In this work, we addressed the critical challenge of specimen-level shortcut learning (\mbox{Same-Specimen-Picture Bias}) in computer-vision-based timber identification. We established that passive dataset partitioning cannot resolve intra-specimen memorization and proposed the Specimen-Invariant Wood Identification Framework, integrating species-conditioned adversarial learning with masked softmax gradient reversal and variational CLUB mutual information minimization. Evaluated across 18 CITES-regulated tropical timber species under a strict 5-fold Leave-One-Specimen-Out protocol, our framework substantially eliminates residual specimen memorization ($\mathrm{SRI} \to 0.118$), achieves superior out-of-specimen generalization ($90.15\%$ Top-1 Accuracy), suppresses hardest-class collapse, and improves model calibration. By ensuring that deep representations reflect authentic biological anatomy rather than surface artifacts, this work establishes a robust technological foundation for automated timber forensics and international CITES trade enforcement.
